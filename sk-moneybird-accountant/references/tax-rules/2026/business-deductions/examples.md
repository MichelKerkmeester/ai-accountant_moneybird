## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### Example 1, hours criterion (urencriterium) in a part year (Official example)

A webhosting business started on 1 July means 6 months as an entrepreneur (ondernemer) in the year, and the taxpayer still has to meet the full 1.225 hours, because the hours are not prorated to the period in which the taxpayer was an entrepreneur. The Chamber of Commerce (KVK) example adds a start on 1 May: eight months remain, about 117 working days, and at 8 hours a day 117 × 8 = 936,00 hours, so extra hours are needed to reach 1.225 hours.

Sources: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/winst/inkomstenbelasting/inkomstenbelasting_voor_ondernemers/voorwaarden_urencriterium and https://www.kvk.nl/starten/de-magische-1225-uur-alles-over-het-urencriterium.

### Example 2, self-employed person's deduction (zelfstandigenaftrek) and SME profit exemption (MKB-winstvrijstelling) at a profit of € 60.000 (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit as an entrepreneur € 60.000,00, the taxpayer has not reached state pension (AOW) age at the start of 2026, the hours criterion is met, no starter's deduction (startersaftrek), no other entrepreneur's allowance.

- Step 1, self-employed person's deduction: € 1.200,00.
- Step 2, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 60.000,00 minus € 1.200,00 = € 58.800,00.
- Step 3, SME profit exemption: € 58.800,00 × 12,7% = € 7.467,60.
- Step 4, taxable profit from business activities (belastbare winst uit onderneming): € 58.800,00 minus € 7.467,60 = € 51.332,40.

### Example 3, starter at a profit of € 60.000 (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit € 60.000,00, the taxpayer has not reached state pension age at the start of 2026, the hours criterion is met, and the prior-years conditions for the starter's deduction are met: the taxpayer was not an entrepreneur in one or more of 2021 to 2025, and the self-employed person's deduction was used at most twice in that period.

- Step 1, self-employed person's deduction: € 1.200,00.
- Step 2, starter's deduction: € 2.123,00.
- Step 3, total entrepreneur's allowance: € 1.200,00 plus € 2.123,00 = € 3.323,00.
- Step 4, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 60.000,00 minus € 3.323,00 = € 56.677,00.
- Step 5, SME profit exemption: € 56.677,00 × 12,7% = € 7.197,979, rounded to € 7.197,98.
- Step 6, taxable profit from business activities: € 56.677,00 minus € 7.197,98 = € 49.479,02.

### Example 4, state pension age at the start of 2026 (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit € 60.000,00, the taxpayer had reached state pension age before 1 January 2026, the hours criterion is met, and the starter conditions for the starter's deduction are met.

- Step 1, self-employed person's deduction at state pension age: € 600,00.
- Step 2, starter's deduction at state pension age: € 1.062,00.
- Step 3, total entrepreneur's allowance: € 600,00 plus € 1.062,00 = € 1.662,00.
- Step 4, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 60.000,00 minus € 1.662,00 = € 58.338,00.
- Step 5, SME profit exemption: € 58.338,00 × 12,7% = € 7.408,926, rounded to € 7.408,93.
- Step 6, taxable profit from business activities: € 58.338,00 minus € 7.408,93 = € 50.929,07.

### Example 5, deduction for assisting partner (meewerkaftrek) at 1.750 hours and at 875 hours (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit € 60.000,00, the entrepreneur meets the hours criterion, the partner works in the business without pay. This example isolates the deduction for assisting partner, so no other part of the entrepreneur's allowance is included.

- At 1.750 hours or more the band is 4%: € 60.000,00 × 4% = € 2.400,00 deduction for assisting partner.
- Profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 60.000,00 minus € 2.400,00 = € 57.600,00.
- SME profit exemption: € 57.600,00 × 12,7% = € 7.315,20.
- Taxable profit from business activities: € 57.600,00 minus € 7.315,20 = € 50.284,80.
- Boundary check at exactly 875 hours: the statute band runs from 875 hours to under 1.225 hours, so the rate is 2%, not 1,25%. At a profit of € 60.000,00 that is € 60.000,00 × 2% = € 1.200,00.

### Example 6, starter's deduction for disabled entrepreneurs (startersaftrek bij arbeidsongeschiktheid) (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit € 20.000,00, the taxpayer was not an entrepreneur in one or more of 2021 to 2025, has a right to a WIA benefit in 2026, does not meet the hours criterion but meets the reduced hours criterion (verlaagd urencriterium) with at least 800 and not more than 1.225 hours, has not reached state pension age at the start of 2026, and the deduction was not applied in the five preceding years.

- Step 1, starter's deduction for disabled entrepreneurs: € 12.000,00, not more than the profit of € 20.000,00, so € 12.000,00.
- Step 2, no self-employed person's deduction, because the hours criterion is not met. The reduced hours criterion is a condition only of the starter's deduction for disabled entrepreneurs, not of the self-employed person's deduction.
- Step 3, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 20.000,00 minus € 12.000,00 = € 8.000,00.
- Step 4, SME profit exemption: € 8.000,00 × 12,7% = € 1.016,00. The SME profit exemption still applies, because the statute sets no hours criterion for it.
- Step 5, taxable profit from business activities: € 8.000,00 minus € 1.016,00 = € 6.984,00.
- Variant with profit € 5.000,00 and no earlier use: the deduction is capped at the profit, so € 5.000,00.

### Example 7, deduction for research and development work (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit € 60.000,00, the taxpayer has not reached state pension age at the start of 2026, the hours criterion is met, and the taxpayer holds an R&D declaration (S&O-verklaring) from the Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO) and made at least 500 hours of research and development work.

- Step 1, self-employed person's deduction: € 1.200,00.
- Step 2, deduction for research and development work: € 15.979,00.
- Step 3, total entrepreneur's allowance: € 1.200,00 plus € 15.979,00 = € 17.179,00.
- Step 4, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 60.000,00 minus € 17.179,00 = € 42.821,00.
- Step 5, SME profit exemption: € 42.821,00 × 12,7% = € 5.438,267, rounded to € 5.438,27.
- Step 6, taxable profit from business activities: € 42.821,00 minus € 5.438,27 = € 37.382,73.
- If the starter conditions for the R&D increase are also met, the total entrepreneur's allowance would be € 1.200,00 plus € 15.979,00 plus € 7.996,00 = € 25.175,00.

### Example 8, rate adjustment (tariefsaanpassing) (Calculated example)

Case 1. Inputs: taxable box 1 income after the deductions is € 80.000,00 and the entrepreneur's allowance plus SME profit exemption together are € 10.000,00.

- Step 1, income plus the deductions: € 80.000,00 plus € 10.000,00 = € 90.000,00.
- Step 2, excess over € 78.426,00: € 90.000,00 minus € 78.426,00 = € 11.574,00.
- Step 3, 11,94% of the excess: € 11.574,00 × 11,94% = € 1.381,9356, rounded to € 1.381,94.
- Step 4, cap: 11,94% of the deductions, € 10.000,00 × 11,94% = € 1.194,00.
- Step 5, the tax rise is the lower of the two, so € 1.194,00.

Case 2. Inputs: taxable box 1 income after the deductions is € 70.000,00 and the deductions are € 5.000,00. Income plus deductions is € 75.000,00, which does not exceed € 78.426,00, so there is no rate adjustment.

### Example 9, discontinuation deduction (stakingsaftrek) (Calculated example)

Inputs: profit from the cessation of one whole business € 5.000,00, no discontinuation deduction was used in earlier years, and no other entrepreneur's allowance applies in this example.

- Step 1, discontinuation deduction: at most € 3.630,00 and at most the profit of € 5.000,00, so € 3.630,00.
- Step 2, profit after the entrepreneur's allowance: € 5.000,00 minus € 3.630,00 = € 1.370,00.
- Step 3, SME profit exemption: € 1.370,00 × 12,7% = € 173,99.
- Step 4, taxable profit from business activities: € 1.370,00 minus € 173,99 = € 1.196,01.
- Earlier use: the maximum of € 3.630,00 is available once in a lifetime and is reduced, not below nil, by a discontinuation deduction enjoyed in earlier years. Earlier use does not remove the discontinuation deduction for a later cessation, it reduces the remaining lifetime maximum by the amount used. If € 1.000,00 was used in an earlier year, the maximum for a later cessation is € 3.630,00 minus € 1.000,00 = € 2.630,00. An unused remainder can be used at a later cessation.

### Example 10, carry-forward from 2025 to 2026 (Official example)

The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) carry-forward example: a profit of € 1.500,00 in 2025 against a self-employed person's deduction of € 2.470,00, so the deduction in 2025 is limited to € 1.500,00 and € 970,00 is an unused self-employed person's deduction (niet-gerealiseerde zelfstandigenaftrek) (€ 2.470,00 minus € 1.500,00 = € 970,00). In 2026 the profit is € 50.000,00 and the self-employed person's deduction is € 1.200,00. The offset is at most the profit minus that year's self-employed person's deduction, so at most € 50.000,00 minus € 1.200,00 = € 48.800,00. The full € 970,00 fits within that cap: € 48.800,00 minus € 970,00 = € 47.830,00. The unused amount remaining after 2026 is € 0,00.

### Example 11, FISIN 2026 carry-forward example as printed (Official example)

FISIN 2026 section 8.4.3 prints a carry-forward example with typos in the year labels and amounts. The printed figures are:

- Year 1: profit € 3.000,00 against a self-employed person's deduction of € 3.750,00, so € 750,00 is unused (€ 3.750,00 minus € 3.000,00 = € 750,00).
- Year 2: a loss of € 25.000,00 and € 2.740,00 unused. The € 2.740,00 is an example typo, the correct amount for that year is € 2.470,00. The printed balance (saldo) is € 3.490,00, which is € 750,00 plus the printed € 2.740,00 (€ 750,00 plus € 2.740,00 = € 3.490,00). With the correct € 2.470,00 the balance would be € 3.220,00 (€ 750,00 plus € 2.470,00 = € 3.220,00).
- Year 3: profit € 100.000,00 minus the self-employed person's deduction of € 1.200,00 gives € 98.800,00. The printed example subtracts the printed balance of € 3.490,00 and gives € 95.310,00 (€ 98.800,00 minus € 3.490,00 = € 95.310,00). With the corrected balance of € 3.220,00 it would give € 95.580,00 (€ 98.800,00 minus € 3.220,00 = € 95.580,00).

This example is reproduced as printed. The corrected amounts are not part of the printed FISIN example.
