## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

No official examples were found in the checked sources for 2026, so every example below is a `Calculated example` built from the section 2 figures.

### Example 1, Vpb at two profit levels

`Calculated example`. A BV has a taxable amount (belastbaar bedrag) of € 300.000,00 for 2026.

Step 1, first bracket: € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00.
Step 2, the part above the first bracket: € 300.000,00 - € 200.000,00 = € 100.000,00.
Step 3, tax on that part: € 100.000,00 x 25,8% = € 25.800,00.
Step 4, total: € 38.000,00 + € 25.800,00 = € 63.800,00.
Step 5, check against the art. 22 formula: € 38.000,00 + 25,8% x € 100.000,00 = € 38.000,00 + € 25.800,00 = € 63.800,00. The effective rate is € 63.800,00 / € 300.000,00 = 21,27% (rounded).

A second BV at exactly € 200.000,00 pays € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00. A third BV at € 1.000.000,00 pays € 38.000,00 + 25,8% x € 800.000,00 = € 38.000,00 + € 206.400,00 = € 244.400,00.

### Example 2, generic interest deduction limitation (generieke renteaftrekbeperking)

`Calculated example`. A BV has an adjusted profit (gecorrigeerde winst) of € 4.000.000,00 and a balance (saldo) of interest of € 2.000.000,00. The balance of interest is the interest costs on loans minus the interest income.

Step 1, percentage leg: € 4.000.000,00 x 24,5% = € 980.000,00.
Step 2, amount leg: € 1.000.000,00.
Step 3, the threshold is the higher of the two: € 1.000.000,00.
Step 4, the balance of interest is deductible up to € 1.000.000,00, so € 2.000.000,00 - € 1.000.000,00 = € 1.000.000,00 is not deductible and carries forward.

Second case, adjusted profit € 10.000.000,00 and balance of interest € 3.000.000,00.

Step 1, percentage leg: € 10.000.000,00 x 24,5% = € 2.450.000,00.
Step 2, amount leg: € 1.000.000,00.
Step 3, the threshold is the higher of the two: € 2.450.000,00.
Step 4, not deductible: € 3.000.000,00 - € 2.450.000,00 = € 550.000,00, which carries forward.

### Example 3, loss offsetting (verliesverrekening)

`Calculated example`. A BV has a profit of € 3.000.000,00 in 2026 and a carry-forward loss from 2023.

Step 1, set-off ceiling: € 1.000.000,00 + 50% x (€ 3.000.000,00 - € 1.000.000,00) = € 1.000.000,00 + 50% x € 2.000.000,00 = € 1.000.000,00 + € 1.000.000,00 = € 2.000.000,00.
Step 2, the loss is set off up to € 2.000.000,00. If the loss is € 2.500.000,00, € 2.000.000,00 is used in 2026 and € 2.500.000,00 - € 2.000.000,00 = € 500.000,00 carries forward.
Step 3, taxable amount (belastbaar bedrag) for 2026: € 3.000.000,00 - € 2.000.000,00 = € 1.000.000,00.
Step 4, Vpb on € 1.000.000,00: first bracket € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00, then € 800.000,00 x 25,8% = € 206.400,00, total € 38.000,00 + € 206.400,00 = € 244.400,00.

If the loss were € 500.000,00, all of it would be set off because € 500.000,00 is below the € 2.000.000,00 ceiling. A loss of 2026 itself may also be carried back one year, to 2025.

### Example 4, innovation box (innovatiebox) effective rate

`Calculated example`. A smaller taxpayer made a qualifying asset itself and holds an R&D declaration (S&O-verklaring). The qualifying advantage is € 500.000,00 and falls in the top bracket, so without the innovation box it would be taxed at 25,8%.

Step 1, without the box: € 500.000,00 x 25,8% = € 129.000,00.
Step 2, with the 9/H fraction the effective rate is 9%: € 500.000,00 x 9% = € 45.000,00.
Step 3, the saving against the rate without the box is € 129.000,00 - € 45.000,00 = € 84.000,00.

### Example 5, innovation box flat rate (forfait)

`Calculated example`. A taxpayer who made a qualifying asset in the year or the two preceding years chooses the art. 12bd flat rate.

Case A, profit before art. 12b of € 80.000,00: € 80.000,00 x 25% = € 20.000,00, which is below the € 25.000,00 maximum, so the flat rate is € 20.000,00.
Case B, profit before art. 12b of € 200.000,00: € 200.000,00 x 25% = € 50.000,00, capped at € 25.000,00.
In both cases the profit before art. 12b must be positive for the flat rate to apply.

### Example 6, customary salary (gebruikelijk loon) and the escape

`Calculated example`. A director and substantial shareholder (DGA) works for his BV. The comparable wage is € 55.000,00, the highest wage of the other employees in the entity and related entities is € 50.000,00, and the 2026 standard amount (normbedrag) is € 58.000,00.

Step 1, the three amounts are € 55.000,00, € 50.000,00 and € 58.000,00.
Step 2, the highest of them is € 58.000,00, so the wage must be at least € 58.000,00.
Step 3, the escape of art. 12a lid 2: the highest amount of € 58.000,00 exceeds the comparable wage of € 55.000,00, so the wage is the comparable wage, € 55.000,00.

Floor case, if the wage for all work in the entity and related entities is € 4.500,00, the rule does not apply because € 4.500,00 is not more than € 5.000,00.

### Example 7, Zvw for the two DGA routes

`Calculated example`. A director and substantial shareholder has a wage of € 100.000,00, above the maximum contribution income (bijdrage-inkomen) of € 79.409,00.

Case A, director and substantial shareholder not insured for the employee insurance schemes (werknemersverzekeringen).
Step 1, the contribution is € 79.409,00 x 4,85% = € 3.851,3365, rounded to € 3.851,34.
Step 2, the wage above € 79.409,00 does not increase the contribution.
Step 3, the checked 2026 material states that this director and substantial shareholder pays the contribution (bijdrage) himself, collected by withholding (inhouding) on net wage (nettoloon) or by assessment (aanslag), and the BV pays no employer levy (werkgeversheffing) and no premiums (premies) employee insurance schemes for him.

Case B, director and substantial shareholder insured for the employee insurance schemes.
Step 1, the BV pays the employer levy of € 79.409,00 x 6,10% = € 4.843,949, rounded to € 4.843,95.

The 2026 Payroll Taxes Handbook (Handboek Loonheffingen) was not read, so the 2026 collection route rests on the 2025 Handbook (Handboek) and the 2026 table.

### Example 8, box 2 on a dividend

`Calculated example`. A director and substantial shareholder receives a dividend of € 100.000,00, above the first bracket of € 68.843,00.

Step 1, the part above the bracket: € 100.000,00 - € 68.843,00 = € 31.157,00.
Step 2, tax on that part: € 31.157,00 x 31% = € 9.658,67.
Step 3, per the art. 2.12 table, the tax is the column III amount of € 16.866,00 plus € 9.658,67 = € 26.524,67.
Step 4, note on the table. € 68.843,00 x 24,5% = € 16.866,535, and € 16.866,535 rounded down to whole euros is € 16.866,00, the amount printed in column III. A separate calculation with the exact 24,5% amount throughout would give € 16.866,535 + € 9.658,67 = € 26.525,205, rounded to € 26.525,21. The example follows the printed amount, so the tax is € 26.524,67.

### Example 9, dividend tax (dividendbelasting)

`Calculated example`. A BV pays a dividend of € 100.000,00 to its shareholder, an investment dividend (beleggingsdividend) to a Dutch shareholder.

Step 1, withholding: € 100.000,00 x 15% = € 15.000,00.
Step 2, the return and the payment are due within 1 month after the day the dividend is made available.
Step 3, an entity offsets the € 15.000,00 in the corporate income tax return (aangifte Vpb), while an individual offsets it in the income tax return (inkomstenbelasting).

If instead the BV bears the tax itself, art. 6 lid 1 grosses up the proceeds (opbrengst): € 100.000,00 x 100/85 = € 117.647,0588, rounded to € 117.647,06. The 15% tax on the grossed-up amount is € 117.647,0588 x 15% = € 17.647,0588, rounded to € 17.647,06.

If the shareholder is a Dutch entity with a holding of 5% or more, the participation dividend (deelnemingsdividend) is usually exempt from withholding under the participation exemption (deelnemingsvrijstelling), and no return is needed for a fully exempt payment unless the company is invited to file.

### Example 10, buyback of own shares (inkoop van eigen aandelen)

`Calculated example`. A BV buys back its own shares. The buyback price is € 50.000,00 and the average paid-up capital on those shares is € 20.000,00.

Step 1, taxable base: € 50.000,00 - € 20.000,00 = € 30.000,00.
Step 2, dividend tax: € 30.000,00 x 15% = € 4.500,00.
Step 3, if the company bears the tax itself and the art. 4c facility for listed companies does not apply, the 100/85 gross-up gives € 30.000,00 x 100/85 = € 35.294,1176, rounded to € 35.294,12. The 15% tax on the grossed-up base is € 35.294,1176 x 15% = € 5.294,1176, rounded to € 5.294,12.

### Example 11, conditional withholding tax (conditionele bronbelasting)

`Calculated example`. A BV pays € 200.000,00 of royalties to a related entity in a low-tax jurisdiction.

Step 1, the rate is the highest art. 22 rate, so 25,8% for 2026.
Step 2, the tax is € 200.000,00 x 25,8% = € 51.600,00.

The same rate applies to in-scope interest, and to dividends since the 2024 version of the Withholding Tax Act 2021 (Wet bronbelasting 2021).

### Example 12, fiscal unity (fiscale eenheid)

`Calculated example`. A parent company (moeder) holds at least 95% of the shares in its subsidiary (dochter), and the two form a fiscal unity for the whole of 2026. The parent company has a profit of € 400.000,00 and the subsidiary has a loss of € 150.000,00.

Step 1, combined taxable amount (belastbaar bedrag): € 400.000,00 - € 150.000,00 = € 250.000,00.
Step 2, first bracket: € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00.
Step 3, the part above the bracket: € 250.000,00 - € 200.000,00 = € 50.000,00, tax € 50.000,00 x 25,8% = € 12.900,00.
Step 4, total Vpb for the fiscal unity: € 38.000,00 + € 12.900,00 = € 50.900,00.
Step 5, without the fiscal unity the parent company alone would pay € 38.000,00 + 25,8% x € 200.000,00 = € 38.000,00 + € 51.600,00 = € 89.600,00. The difference is € 89.600,00 - € 50.900,00 = € 38.700,00.

### Example 13, annual accounts (jaarrekening) size class

`Calculated example`. A BV has a balance sheet total of € 500.000,00, a net turnover of € 800.000,00 and 8 employees at the balance date, with the same picture one year earlier.

Step 1, assets of € 500.000,00 against the micro bound of € 450.000,00, not met.
Step 2, turnover of € 800.000,00 against the micro bound of € 900.000,00, met.
Step 3, 8 employees against the micro bound of fewer than 10 employees, met.
Step 4, two of the three criteria are met on two consecutive balance dates, so the BV is a micro company.

The accounts must be deposited within eight days after adoption (vaststelling) and at the latest twelve months after the financial year ends, and from financial year (boekjaar) 2025 all legal persons file through SBR.

### Example 14, discontinuation deduction (stakingsaftrek) on a taxable contribution (ruisende inbreng)

`Calculated example`. A sole trader brings the business into a BV without the art. 3.65 facility, so the change is a cessation. The profit from business discontinuation (stakingswinst) is € 40.000,00.

Step 1, the discontinuation deduction of art. 3.79 is at most € 3.630,00 and is used once in a lifetime.
Step 2, remaining profit from business discontinuation: € 40.000,00 - € 3.630,00 = € 36.370,00.
Step 3, the income tax is computed on the remaining € 36.370,00, and an extra annuity (lijfrente) premium deduction is available (art. 3.129).

Hidden reserves realised by a direct withdrawal (onttrekking) before a tax-neutral conversion (geruisloze omzetting) do not qualify for the discontinuation deduction but may go into an annuity.
