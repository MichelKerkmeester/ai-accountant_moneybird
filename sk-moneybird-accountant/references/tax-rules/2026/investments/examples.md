## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### Example 1. KIA in the 28% band (Calculated example)

A sole trader invests € 60.000 in business assets (bedrijfsmiddelen) in 2026. No asset is excluded and every asset costs more than € 450.

Step 1. The investment of € 60.000 is above € 2.900 and at or below € 71.683, so the second band applies at 28%.
Step 2. KIA = 28% × € 60.000 = € 16.800.
Step 3. Check against the next band. 28% × € 71.683 = € 20.071,24, which is just below the fixed € 20.072 of the third band, as the table states.

The KIA is € 16.800. Figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 2. KIA in the fixed band (Calculated example)

The same sole trader invests € 100.000 instead.

Step 1. € 100.000 is above € 71.683 and at or below € 132.746.
Step 2. The third band gives a fixed amount, so KIA = € 20.072.
Step 3. The 28% of the second band is not used here, because the table is a step function.

The KIA is € 20.072 and not € 28.000. Figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 3. KIA in the fourth band (Calculated example)

The same sole trader invests € 150.000.

Step 1. € 150.000 is above € 132.746 and at or below € 398.236.
Step 2. Part above € 132.746 = € 150.000 minus € 132.746 = € 17.254.
Step 3. 7,56% × € 17.254 = € 1.304,4024, rounded to € 1.304,40.
Step 4. KIA = € 20.072 minus € 1.304,40 = € 18.767,60.

The KIA is € 18.767,60. Figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 4. KIA above the top amount (Calculated example)

The same sole trader invests € 400.000.

Step 1. € 400.000 is above € 398.236.
Step 2. In the top band the deduction is nil, so KIA = € 0.

The KIA is nil, even though individual assets still cost more than € 450. Figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 5. KIA in a partnership (samenwerkingsverband)

A general partnership (vof) has two partners (vennoten). The vof invests € 40.000 in 2026. One partner (vennoot) also invests € 10.000 in his own business (onderneming).

Step 1. Add the partnership investments and the own investments, € 40.000 + € 10.000 = € 50.000.
Step 2. Apply the table to the sum, € 50.000 is in the 28% band, so 28% × € 50.000 = € 14.000.
Step 3. Multiply by the own investment divided by the sum, € 10.000 / € 50.000 = 0,2.
Step 4. KIA for this partner = € 14.000 × 0,2 = € 2.800.

The KIA is € 2.800. Rule from https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353/2026-01-01 art. 3.41 lid 3, figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 6. EIA combined with the KIA (Calculated example)

A sole trader buys one new machine for € 100.000 in 2026. The machine is on the Energy List (Energielijst), it was not used before, and it is reported to RVO within 3 months.

Step 1. The machine is above the € 2.500 EIA floor and far below the € 153.000.000 maximum.
Step 2. EIA = 40% × € 100.000 = € 40.000.
Step 3. The same machine may also enter the KIA base. The total investment in business assets for 2026 is € 100.000, so the KIA is the fixed € 20.072 of the third band.
Step 4. The MIA cannot be claimed on this same machine, because the EIA and the MIA cannot be combined for one asset.

The deductions are € 40.000 EIA plus € 20.072 KIA. Figures from section 2, EIA percentage, EIA and MIA maximum, EIA and MIA minimum and KIA table rows.

### Example 7. MIA and Vamil on one asset (Calculated example)

A sole trader buys a new asset of € 40.000 in 2026. Its Environmental List (Milieulijst) code starts with B, it is not used before, and the investment is reported to RVO within 3 months.

Step 1. Code B maps to category (categorie) III at 27%.
Step 2. MIA = 27% × € 40.000 = € 10.800.
Step 3. The € 40.000 is above the € 2.500 floor and below the € 153.000.000 maximum.
Step 4. Code B also qualifies for 75% Vamil, so 75% × € 40.000 = € 30.000 may be arbitrarily depreciated, and the remaining € 10.000 follows the normal depreciation schedule.

The MIA is € 10.800 and € 30.000 of the cost can be arbitrarily depreciated under the Vamil. Figures from section 2, MIA percentages, MIA and Vamil code mapping, EIA and MIA minimum and Vamil percentage rows.

### Example 8. Ordinary depreciation caps (Calculated example)

A sole trader buys a machine for € 40.000 and acquires goodwill for € 30.000 in 2026.

Step 1. Machine, at most 20% per year, so 20% × € 40.000 = € 8.000 per year, which means at least 5 years.
Step 2. Goodwill, at most 10% per year, so 10% × € 30.000 = € 3.000 per year, which means at least 10 years.

Both assets need a depreciation schedule that stays within these yearly caps. Figures from section 2, ordinary depreciation caps row.

### Example 9. Building and minimum value (bodemwaarde)

A sole trader owns a building that is a business asset (bedrijfsmiddel). The book value at the start of 2026 is € 400.000 and the WOZ value (WOZ-waarde) 2026 is € 300.000.

The building was taken into use before 2021 and has been depreciated for more than 3 years, so the transition exception does not apply.

Step 1. The minimum value is the WOZ value, € 300.000.
Step 2. The book value of € 400.000 is above the minimum value, so depreciation is allowed.
Step 3. The maximum for 2026 is the difference, € 400.000 minus € 300.000 = € 100.000.
Step 4. After a depreciation of € 100.000 the book value equals the minimum value and no further depreciation on the building is possible until the WOZ value changes.

Figures from section 2, minimum value row.

### Example 10. Disinvestment addition (desinvesteringsbijtelling)

A sole trader buys a machine for € 50.000 in 2026 and claims the KIA. The investment falls in the 28% band, so the investment deduction (investeringsaftrek) percentage taken is 28%. He sells the machine in 2028 for € 30.000.

Step 1. The sale is within five years after the start of the calendar year of the investment, 2026.
Step 2. The transfer price of € 30.000 is above the € 2.900 threshold.
Step 3. Disinvestment addition = 28% × € 30.000 = € 8.400.
Step 4. Check the cap. The investment amount over which deduction (aftrek) was taken is € 50.000, and € 8.400 is below that, so the full € 8.400 stands.

The 2028 profit includes a disinvestment addition of € 8.400. Figures from section 2, KIA table and disinvestment addition threshold rows.

### Example 11. Starter's arbitrary depreciation (starters willekeurige afschrijving)

A starter meets the starter's deduction (startersaftrek) conditions in 2026 and buys a machine for € 30.000 in 2026. The asset qualifies for investment deduction.

Step 1. Check the yearly cap. The total costs stay at € 30.000, which is below € 398.236, so art. 8 of the URWA does not limit the arbitrary depreciation (willekeurige afschrijving) on this machine (https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0012035/2025-01-01/0 art. 7 to 9).
Step 2. The arbitrary depreciation must fit within the period of art. 3.38, five years from the start of 2026.
Step 3. The minimum total investment of the KIA table, € 2.900, does not apply to the arbitrary depreciation, so a small total does not block the scheme (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/winst/inkomstenbelasting/inkomstenbelasting_voor_ondernemers/afschrijving/willekeurige_afschrijving/startende_ondernemer).

Figures from section 2, starter's arbitrary depreciation cap and KIA table rows.

### Example 12. Asset not yet in use, payment cap (Calculated example)

A sole trader orders a machine for € 20.000 in 2026 and pays € 8.000 in 2026. The machine is not in use at the end of 2026.

Step 1. The investment is not yet in use at the end of the calendar year, so the KIA base is limited to the payments made, € 8.000.
Step 2. € 8.000 is above € 2.900 and at or below € 71.683, so the 28% band applies.
Step 3. KIA for 2026 = 28% × € 8.000 = € 2.240.
Step 4. The remaining € 12.000 of the cost follows in later years, when further payments are made or the asset is in use.

The rule is in https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353/2026-01-01 art. 3.44 lid 1 and applies to the KIA, the EIA and the MIA. At cessation (staking) of the business the full investment deduction is taken in the year of cessation (https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353/2026-01-01 art. 3.44 lid 2). Figures from section 2, KIA table row.

### Example 13. Truck examples ending in 2026 (Official example)

The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) truck examples on the page about arbitrary depreciation for starters end in 2026. The 2024 truck example ends in 2026 with a revaluation (herwaardering) from € 10.000 to € 120.000, a book profit (boekwinst) of € 110.000 and € 20.000 normal depreciation in 2026. Check, € 120.000 minus € 10.000 = € 110.000. The 2021 truck example ends in 2026 with no revaluation.
