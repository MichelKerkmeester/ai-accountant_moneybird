## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

All inputs below are invented round numbers. Calculated examples use only the section 2 figures and the checked material. Every product is shown with two decimals. No step in these examples requires rounding, so no rounding step is marked.

### 4.1 Calculated example: threshold for mixed costs (gemengde kosten drempel) against the 80% election

A sole trader has € 6.000 of mixed costs (gemengde kosten) in 2027, made up of business lunches (zakenlunches) and receptions (recepties).

Step 1, threshold route with the base amount of € 5.700 from the 2027 text: deductible is € 6.000 minus € 5.700 = € 300,00. The effective 2027 threshold is not published yet, so this step uses the base amount only.

Step 2, election route per art. 3.15 lid 5: 80% of € 6.000 = € 4.800,00 deductible.

Step 3, the election in the return gives the higher deduction here, € 4.800,00 against € 300,00. Check: 0,80 times € 6.000 = € 4.800,00.

### 4.2 Calculated example: conference travel cap

A sole trader spends € 2.000 on travel and stay for a conference (congres) in 2027.

Step 1, art. 3.16 lid 8 caps the deduction at € 1.500. Deductible € 1.500,00, not deductible (niet aftrekbaar) € 2.000 minus € 1.500 = € 500,00.

Step 2, if the nature of the work makes attendance necessary, the cap does not apply and the full € 2.000,00 is deductible.

### 4.3 Calculated example: mileage allowance (kilometervergoeding) with a private car (privéauto)

A sole trader drives 12.000 business kilometres in 2027 in a car that belongs to the private assets (privévermogen). Fuel (brandstof), insurance (verzekering), toll and parking are not deductible separately.

Step 1, enacted 2027 text: 12.000 km times € 0,23 = € 2.760,00.

Step 2, if Tax Plan 2027 (Belastingplan 2027) is adopted: 12.000 km times € 0,25 = € 3.000,00.

Step 3, the difference is € 3.000,00 minus € 2.760,00 = € 240,00. Until adoption, use € 2.760,00. Check: 0,23 times 12.000 = 2.760,00 and 0,25 times 12.000 = 3.000,00.

### 4.4 Calculated example: private-use addition (bijtelling) at 22% with the car costs (autokosten) cap

A car classified as a business asset (bedrijfsmiddel) (ondernemingsvermogen) has a catalogue price (catalogusprijs) of € 30.000 and is used privately for more than 500 km in 2027.

Step 1, 22% of € 30.000 = € 6.600,00 withdrawal (onttrekking).

Step 2, if the total car costs (autokosten) including depreciation are € 10.000, the full € 6.600,00 is taken into account.

Step 3, if the total car costs including depreciation are € 5.000, art. 3.20 lid 4 caps the withdrawal at the total car costs including depreciation, so € 5.000,00 is taken into account.

Check: 0,22 times 30.000 = 6.600,00.

### 4.5 Calculated example: pre-2017 car

A car first admitted on 1 June 2016 has a catalogue price of € 20.000 and is used privately for more than 500 km in 2027.

Step 1, art. 10a.4 gives at least 25% instead of 22%, so 25% of € 20.000 = € 5.000,00.

Step 2, without art. 10a.4 the 22% would have given € 4.400,00. The difference is € 600,00. Check: 0,25 times 20.000 = 5.000,00 and 0,22 times 20.000 = 4.400,00.

### 4.6 Calculated example: part-year availability and the 500 km rule

A car with a catalogue price of € 24.000 is available to the sole trader for 5 months in 2027 and is used privately for more than 500 km.

Step 1, pro-rate the yearly amount: 5/12 times 22% times € 24.000. First 22% of € 24.000 = € 5.280,00.

Step 2, 5/12 of € 5.280,00 = € 2.200,00.

Step 3, the 500 km rule applies to the whole year. Private km in a part year are converted to a yearly basis. If trip registration (rittenregistratie) shows 200 private km in the 5 months, the yearly basis is 200 times 12 / 5 = 480,00 km, which is at most 500, so the withdrawal is nil. If it shows 480 private km, the yearly basis is 480 times 12 / 5 = 1.152,00 km, which is more than 500, so the withdrawal stays € 2.200,00.

Check: 5.280,00 times 5 = 26.400,00 and 26.400,00 / 12 = 2.200,00. Also 200 times 12 = 2.400,00 and 2.400,00 / 5 = 480,00. And 480 times 12 = 5.760,00 and 5.760,00 / 5 = 1.152,00.

### 4.7 Calculated example: youngtimer, enacted text against the bill

A car was first used between 17 and 25 years ago, so it is older than the bill threshold but not older than the enacted 2027 threshold of 25 years. Its WEV is € 6.000.

Step 1, under the enacted 2027 text art. 3.20 lid 5 b still uses more than 15 years for the WEV switch, so the WEV of € 6.000 replaces the catalogue price. The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) applies 16 years from 2026, and the car is older than 17 years, so the WEV applies under both readings.

Step 2, enacted 2027 text: the car is not older than 25 years, so the 35% youngtimer rate does not apply. The standard 22% applies to the WEV: 0,22 times € 6.000 = € 1.320,00.

Step 3, Tax Plan 2027: the threshold would be 17 years in 2027, so 35% of € 6.000 = € 2.100,00.

Step 4, the difference is € 2.100,00 minus € 1.320,00 = € 780,00. Check: 0,22 times 6.000 = 1.320,00 and 0,35 times 6.000 = 2.100,00.

### 4.8 Official example: reclassification after the youngtimer change

Knowledge group case (Kennisgroep casus) KG:213:2026:1 describes a youngtimer bought in 2024 as freely classifiable assets (keuzevermogen), where the move to 25 years from 2027 can justify a new labelling choice (etiketkeuze) after 1 January 2027. Official example, source: [KG:213:2026:1](https://kennisgroepen.belastingdienst.nl/publicaties/kg21320261-heretikettering-bij-wetswijziging-privegebruik-auto/).

### 4.9 Calculated example: zero-emission car (zero-emissie auto) first admitted in 2027

A zero-emission car with a catalogue price of € 40.000 is first admitted in 2027 and used privately for more than 500 km.

Step 1, 20% on the part up to € 30.000: 0,20 times € 30.000 = € 6.000,00.

Step 2, 22% on the part above € 30.000: 0,22 times € 10.000 = € 2.200,00.

Step 3, total withdrawal € 6.000,00 plus € 2.200,00 = € 8.200,00.

Check with the euro cap: 22% of € 40.000 = € 8.800,00 and the reduction cap is € 600,00, so € 8.800,00 minus € 600,00 = € 8.200,00.

### 4.10 Calculated example: bicycle (fiets) of the business

A business e-bike has a recommended retail price (consumentenadviesprijs) of € 2.500 and is also used privately.

Step 1, 7% of € 2.500 = € 175,00 withdrawal, capped at the costs borne.

Step 2, if the bicycle is parked at the home address only incidentally, the withdrawal is nil. Check: 0,07 times 2.500 = 175,00.

### 4.11 Calculated example: laptop and the € 450 rule

A laptop of € 1.800 is business property, used longer than a year, with a residual value (restwaarde) of € 300 and a five-year life.

Step 1, depreciate: € 1.800 minus € 300 = € 1.500,00.

Step 2, per year: € 1.500,00 / 5 = € 300,00.

Step 3, a laptop stand of € 40 is deducted in one go because it is under € 450. If the laptop were used 10% or less for business, it would have to be classified as compulsory private assets (verplicht privévermogen) and would be 0% deductible.

### 4.12 Calculated example: relocation costs (verhuiskosten)

Moving the household contents (inboedel) costs € 1.200.

Step 1, deductible is the cost of moving the household contents plus € 7.750: € 1.200 plus € 7.750 = € 8.950,00. Check: 1.200 plus 7.750 = 8.950,00.

### 4.13 Calculated example: VAT (btw) correction on a private car used for business

A private car with a catalogue price including VAT and bpm of € 40.000 is used for business without conclusive mileage records (zonder sluitende kilometeradministratie), and 4 months of the year are in the business.

Step 1, the year-end correction is 1,5% of € 40.000 = € 600,00.

Step 2, pro rata for 4 months: 4/12 times € 600,00 = € 200,00.

Step 3, the correction is never above the VAT deducted on use and maintenance, so if that VAT is € 150, the correction is € 150,00. Check: 0,015 times 40.000 = 600,00 and 600,00 times 4 / 12 = 200,00.

### 4.14 Calculated example: public transport

A business train trip costs € 96. The full € 96,00 is deductible, and the train ticket or a public transport smart card (OV-chipkaart) trip overview shows the cost.

### 4.15 Calculated example: posts rated at 0% and at 100% for telephone and fines

Step 1, a private telephone subscription of € 30 per month is not deductible. Business calls made through it are deductible, for example € 18 of business call costs in a month.

Step 2, a Wahv traffic fine (verkeersboete) of € 180 is not deductible under art. 3.14 lid 1 and the overview page, which prints Fines 0%.

### 4.16 Calculated example: small businesses scheme (kleineondernemersregeling) (KOR) and the cost base (kostengrondslag)

A sole trader in the KOR buys office supplies for € 1.210 including 21% VAT.

Step 1, the VAT part is € 210,00 and the amount excluding VAT is € 1.000,00. Check: 1.000 times 21% = 210,00 and 1.000 plus 210 = 1.210,00.

Step 2, under the KOR no VAT is deducted, so the deduction for income tax (inkomstenbelasting) is the amount including VAT, € 1.210,00. If the VAT were deductible in a VAT return (btw-aangifte), the income tax deduction would be € 1.000,00.

### 4.17 Calculated example: home workspace (werkruimte) test

A sole trader rents a home for an invented € 1.200 per month and uses one room as an office. The room has no own entrance, no own sanitary facilities and could not be rented to a third party (derde). The room is therefore not a self-contained part of the home according to prevailing views (naar verkeersopvatting zelfstandig) and the workspace costs are 0% deductible. A room with its own entrance, its own sanitary facilities and a realistic rental option, in which the taxpayer earns most of his income, can qualify under art. 3.16 lid 1.

### 4.18 Official example: pseudo-final levy (pseudo-eindheffing)

Official example from Rijksoverheid: a car of € 30.000 gives 12% = € 3.600,00 per year, which is € 300,00 per month. Official example, source: [Rijksoverheid on the car of the zaak](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/belastingen-op-auto-en-motor/ik-krijg-een-auto-van-de-zaak.-welke-belastingen-moet-ik-betalen).
