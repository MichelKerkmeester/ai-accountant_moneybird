## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

The checked material for 2023 lists no official example. Every example below is a `Calculated example` and uses the section 2 figures. Every product is written with two decimals and the rounding step is marked.

### Calculated example: Corporate income tax (Vpb) rate on a profit of € 250.000
Step 1: first bracket (eerste schijf) € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00.
Step 2: part above the bracket € 250.000,00 minus € 200.000,00 = € 50.000,00.
Step 3: tax above the bracket € 38.000,00 plus € 50.000,00 x 25,8% = € 38.000,00 plus € 12.900,00 = € 50.900,00.
Answer: € 50.900,00.

### Calculated example: Corporate income tax rate (vpb-tarief) on a profit of € 60.000
Step 1: the whole profit is in the first bracket.
Step 2: € 60.000,00 x 19% = € 11.400,00.
Answer: € 11.400,00.

### Calculated example: art. 31 apportionment for a book year from 1 July 2022 to 30 June 2023
Step 1: days before 1 January 2023 are July 31, August 31, September 30, October 31, November 30 and December 31, so X = 184.
Step 2: days from 1 January 2023 to 30 June 2023 are January 31, February 28, March 31, April 30, May 31 and June 30, so Y = 181.
Step 3: B = 184 plus 181 = 365.
Step 4: profit € 250.000,00. Old tax To under the 2022 article is € 250.000,00 x 15% = € 37.500,00.
Step 5: new tax Tn under the 2023 article is € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00 plus € 50.000,00 x 25,8% = € 12.900,00, so Tn = € 50.900,00.
Step 6: X/B x To = 184/365 x € 37.500,00 = € 18.904,11 rounded.
Step 7: Y/B x Tn = 181/365 x € 50.900,00 = € 25.240,82 rounded.
Step 8: total = € 18.904,11 plus € 25.240,82 = € 44.144,93, rounded to € 44.145.
Answer: € 44.145.

### Calculated example: loss offsetting (verliesverrekening) with a profit of € 2.500.000 and a loss carryforward of € 2.000.000
Step 1: set-off limit = € 1.000.000,00 plus 50% x (€ 2.500.000,00 minus € 1.000.000,00) = € 1.000.000,00 plus € 750.000,00 = € 1.750.000,00.
Step 2: taxable profit (belastbare winst) = € 2.500.000,00 minus € 1.750.000,00 = € 750.000,00.
Step 3: remaining loss = € 2.000.000,00 minus € 1.750.000,00 = € 250.000,00, carried forward.
Step 4: corporate income tax = € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00.
Step 5: part above the bracket = € 750.000,00 minus € 200.000,00 = € 550.000,00.
Step 6: tax above the bracket = € 38.000,00 plus € 550.000,00 x 25,8% = € 38.000,00 plus € 141.900,00 = € 179.900,00.
Answer: € 179.900,00.

### Calculated example: art. 15b interest deduction limitation (renteaftrekbeperking)
Case A: balance of interest (saldo aan renten) € 1.500.000,00 and adjusted profit (gecorrigeerde winst) € 4.000.000,00.
Step 1: 20% x € 4.000.000,00 = € 800.000,00.
Step 2: higher of € 800.000,00 and € 1.000.000,00 = € 1.000.000,00.
Step 3: deductible balance = € 1.000.000,00.
Step 4: non-deductible balance = € 1.500.000,00 minus € 1.000.000,00 = € 500.000,00, carried forward.
Case B: adjusted profit € 6.000.000,00.
Step 1: 20% x € 6.000.000,00 = € 1.200.000,00.
Step 2: higher of € 1.200.000,00 and € 1.000.000,00 = € 1.200.000,00.
Step 3: deductible balance = € 1.200.000,00.
Step 4: non-deductible balance = € 1.500.000,00 minus € 1.200.000,00 = € 300.000,00, carried forward.
Answer: case A € 500.000,00 non-deductible, case B € 300.000,00 non-deductible.

### Calculated example: innovation box (innovatiebox)
Step 1: qualifying advantage € 100.000,00 x 9% = € 9.000,00.
Step 2: normal corporate income tax on the same amount at the first bracket rate € 100.000,00 x 19% = € 19.000,00.
Step 3: difference = € 19.000,00 minus € 9.000,00 = € 10.000,00.
Flat rate (forfait) case: profit before art. 12b € 80.000,00. The flat rate applies when the profit before art. 12b is positive and the taxpayer made a qualifying asset in the year or one of the two preceding years and chooses the flat rate.
Step 1: 25% x € 80.000,00 = € 20.000,00.
Step 2: maximum € 25.000,00, so the flat rate is € 20.000,00.
Step 3: tax at the effective rate (effectieve tarief) 9% x € 20.000,00 = € 1.800,00.
If the profit before art. 12b were € 200.000,00, 25% is € 50.000,00 but the maximum is € 25.000,00, so the flat rate is € 25.000,00 and the tax is 9% x € 25.000,00 = € 2.250,00.
Answer: € 9.000,00 effective tax on € 100.000,00, or € 1.800,00 flat rate tax on € 80.000,00, or € 2.250,00 flat rate tax on € 200.000,00.

### Calculated example: customary salary (gebruikelijk loon)
Case A: comparable wage (vergelijkbaar loon) € 70.000,00, highest employee wage € 55.000,00, standard amount (normbedrag) € 51.000,00.
Step 1: highest of € 70.000,00, € 55.000,00 and € 51.000,00 = € 70.000,00.
Step 2: the wage must be at least € 70.000,00.
Case B escape: comparable wage € 40.000,00, highest employee wage € 45.000,00, standard amount € 51.000,00.
Step 1: highest amount of b and c = € 51.000,00.
Step 2: € 51.000,00 exceeds the comparable wage of € 40.000,00, so the wage is the comparable wage of € 40.000,00.
Case C floor: if the wage for all work in the body and connected bodies is not more than € 5.000,00, the rule does not apply.
Case D corporate income tax effect: profit before salary € 200.000,00, salary € 70.000,00, profit after salary € 130.000,00, corporate income tax 19% x € 130.000,00 = € 24.700,00.
Answer: at least € 70.000,00 in case A, € 40.000,00 in case B, no rule in case C, and € 24.700,00 corporate income tax in case D.

### Calculated example: Zvw for a director and substantial shareholder (directeur-grootaandeelhouder, DGA)
An uninsured director and substantial shareholder with a salary of € 80.000,00.
Step 1: maximum contribution income (bijdrage-inkomen) = € 66.956,00.
Step 2: Health Insurance Act (Zorgverzekeringswet, Zvw) contribution (bijdrage Zvw) = 5,43% x € 66.956,00 = € 3.635,71 rounded.
Step 3: insured director and substantial shareholder (verzekerde dga) employer levy (werkgeversheffing) Zvw = 6,68% x € 66.956,00 = € 4.472,66 rounded.
Answer: € 3.635,71 for the uninsured director and substantial shareholder, or € 4.472,66 for the insured director and substantial shareholder.

### Calculated example: box 2 and dividend tax (dividendbelasting) on a dividend of € 100.000
Step 1: box 2 tax = 26,9% x € 100.000,00 = € 26.900,00.
Step 2: dividend tax = 15% x € 100.000,00 = € 15.000,00.
Step 3: credit = € 15.000,00.
Step 4: remaining box 2 tax = € 26.900,00 minus € 15.000,00 = € 11.900,00.
Step 5: net cash after dividend tax = € 100.000,00 minus € 15.000,00 = € 85.000,00.
Answer: € 11.900,00 remaining box 2 tax and € 85.000,00 net cash.

### Calculated example: dividend tax gross-up on a dividend of € 50.000
Step 1: grossed-up proceeds (opbrengst) = € 50.000,00 x 100/85 = € 58.823,53 rounded.
Step 2: dividend tax = 15% x € 58.823,53 = € 8.823,53 rounded.
Step 3: the company pays the shareholder € 50.000,00 and pays € 8.823,53 dividend tax.
Answer: € 8.823,53.

Assume the interest payment is within scope.
Step 1: rate = 25,8%.
Step 2: tax = 25,8% x € 200.000,00 = € 51.600,00.
Answer: € 51.600,00.

### Calculated example: annual accounts (jaarrekening) size class
Balance sheet total € 400.000,00, net turnover (netto-omzet) € 800.000,00, 8 employees, the same figures on two consecutive balance dates.
Default values:
Step 1: micro test on both balance dates. Assets € 400.000,00 is more than € 350.000,00. Turnover € 800.000,00 is more than € 700.000,00. Employees 8 is fewer than 10. Only one of the three criteria is met on each date, so the company does not meet the micro criteria on two consecutive balance dates.
Step 2: small test on both balance dates. Assets € 400.000,00 is less than € 6.000.000,00. Turnover € 800.000,00 is less than € 12.000.000,00. Employees 8 is fewer than 50. All three criteria are met on both dates, so the company meets the small criteria on two consecutive balance dates.
Raised values, optional from 1 January 2023 and applied on both balance dates:
Step 3: micro test on both balance dates. Assets € 400.000,00 is less than € 450.000,00. Turnover € 800.000,00 is less than € 900.000,00. Employees 8 is fewer than 10. All three criteria are met on both dates, so the company meets the micro criteria on two consecutive balance dates.
Answer: small under the default values, micro if the raised values are used on both balance dates.

### Calculated example: tax-neutral conversion (geruisloze omzetting) compared with taxable contribution (ruisende inbreng)
Step 1: fiscal book values total € 150.000,00, so the acquisition price (verkrijgingsprijs) of the shares is € 150.000,00.
Step 2: in a tax-neutral conversion the business is deemed not ceased, so no profit from business discontinuation (stakingswinst) arises from the contribution.
Step 3: in a taxable contribution the real value is € 400.000,00 and the book value is € 150.000,00, so the profit from business discontinuation is € 400.000,00 minus € 150.000,00 = € 250.000,00.
Step 4: discontinuation deduction (stakingsaftrek) is at most € 3.630,00.
Step 5: taxable profit from business discontinuation = € 250.000,00 minus € 3.630,00 = € 246.370,00.
Step 6: the rounding credit in the tax-neutral conversion route is at most 5% of the paid-up amount and not more than € 30.000,00. The paid-up amount on the fully paid-up shares for the contributed assets is € 150.000,00, so the credit is at most 5% x € 150.000,00 = € 7.500,00.
Answer: no profit from business discontinuation in the tax-neutral conversion route, € 246.370,00 taxable profit from business discontinuation in the taxable contribution (ruisende) route, and a rounding credit of at most € 7.500,00.

### Calculated example: arbitrary depreciation (willekeurige afschrijving) 2023
Step 1: asset cost € 100.000,00.
Step 2: maximum arbitrary depreciation = 50% x € 100.000,00 = € 50.000,00.
Step 3: the costs must be made in 2023 and the asset must be in use before 1 January 2026.
Answer: € 50.000,00.

### Calculated example: fiscal unity (fiscale eenheid) with mother profit € 300.000 and daughter loss € 100.000
Step 1: combined taxable amount (belastbaar bedrag) = € 300.000,00 minus € 100.000,00 = € 200.000,00.
Step 2: corporate income tax in the fiscal unity = 19% x € 200.000,00 = € 38.000,00.
Step 3: mother alone would pay € 200.000,00 x 19% = € 38.000,00 plus € 100.000,00 x 25,8% = € 25.800,00, so € 63.800,00.
Step 4: difference = € 63.800,00 minus € 38.000,00 = € 25.800,00.
Answer: € 38.000,00 in the fiscal unity, a difference of € 25.800,00.

### Calculated example: participation exemption (deelnemingsvrijstelling) on a dividend of € 50.000
Step 1: the 5% participation (deelneming) means art. 13 lid 1 leaves the advantage out of the profit, so the corporate income tax base (grondslag) effect is € 0,00.
Step 2: without the exemption the corporate income tax at 19% would be € 50.000,00 x 19% = € 9.500,00.
Answer: € 0,00 under the participation exemption, € 9.500,00 without it.

### Calculated example: buy-back of own shares (inkoop van eigen aandelen)
Step 1: buyback price € 100.000,00 minus average paid-up capital (gemiddeld gestort kapitaal) € 20.000,00 = € 80.000,00.
Step 2: dividend tax = 15% x € 80.000,00 = € 12.000,00.
Step 3: if the company bears the tax, gross-up = € 80.000,00 x 100/85 = € 94.117,65 rounded.
Step 4: dividend tax = 15% x € 94.117,65 = € 14.117,65 rounded.
Answer: € 12.000,00, or € 14.117,65 if the company bears the tax.

### Calculated example: Corporate income tax filing dates for a calendar book year 2023
Step 1: file before 1 June 2024.
Step 2: online extension runs to 1 November 2024 and is requested from 1 March to 1 June 2024.
Step 3: a paper request is needed for a broken book year or for more than 5 months and must be filed within 5 months after the book year ends, with reasons.
Answer: 1 June 2024, or 1 November 2024 with the online extension.

### Calculated example: retention periods (bewaartermijnen)
Step 1: general records seven years.
Step 2: records on immovable property (onroerende zaken) nine years following the year in which use began, which is effectively ten years.
Step 3: OSS records ten years after the year of the transaction.
Answer: 7 years for the general records, nine years following the year in which use began (effectively 10 years) for immovable property, and 10 years for OSS records.
