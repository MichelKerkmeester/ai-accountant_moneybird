## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### Example 1. Official example, wage (loon) plus freelance income (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2023/inkomensafhankelijke_bijdrage_zorgverzekeringswet)

Facts: wage of € 40.000 and freelance income of € 30.000 in 2023.

Step 1: the maximum contribution income (bijdrage-inkomen) is € 66.956 (section 2).
Step 2: the wage already bore a Zvw contribution (bijdrage zorgverzekeringswet), so it uses up headroom. Headroom is € 66.956 minus € 40.000 = € 26.956.
Step 3: the source states that the 5,43% applies to € 26.956.
Step 4: the contribution (bijdrage) is € 26.956 x 5,43% = € 1.463,71 (the multiplication is arithmetic added here, the source prints the € 26.956 base, not the euro outcome).

### Example 2. Official example, no headroom left (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2023/inkomensafhankelijke_bijdrage_zorgverzekeringswet)

Facts: wage of € 70.000 and freelance income of € 15.000 in 2023.

Step 1: the maximum contribution income is € 66.956.
Step 2: € 70.000 is above € 66.956, so the wage already exceeds the maximum.
Step 3: remaining headroom is € 0.
Step 4: the source states that no contribution is due on the freelance income.

### Example 3. Calculated example, profit of € 60.000, below state pension age (AOW age)

Facts: profits from business activities (winst uit onderneming) of € 60.000, no wage, below state pension age.

Step 1: profit = € 60.000.
Step 2: self-employed person's deduction (zelfstandigenaftrek) = € 5.030. Profit after the entrepreneur's allowance (ondernemersaftrek) = € 60.000 minus € 5.030 = € 54.970.
Step 3: SME profit exemption (MKB-winstvrijstelling) = 14% x € 54.970 = € 7.695,80.
Step 4: taxable profit (belastbare winst) = € 54.970 minus € 7.695,80 = € 47.274,20.
Step 5: € 47.274,20 is below the maximum of € 66.956, so the contribution income is € 47.274,20.
Step 6: contribution = € 47.274,20 x 5,43% = € 2.566,99.

### Example 4. Calculated example, profit of € 100.000, the maximum applies

Facts: profit from business of € 100.000, no wage, below state pension age.

Step 1: profit = € 100.000.
Step 2: self-employed person's deduction = € 5.030. Profit after the entrepreneur's allowance = € 100.000 minus € 5.030 = € 94.970.
Step 3: SME profit exemption = 14% x € 94.970 = € 13.295,80.
Step 4: taxable profit = € 94.970 minus € 13.295,80 = € 81.674,20.
Step 5: € 81.674,20 is above the maximum of € 66.956, so the contribution income is capped at € 66.956.
Step 6: contribution = € 66.956 x 5,43% = € 3.635,71.

### Example 5. Calculated example, state pension age (AOW age), profit of € 40.000

Facts: profit from business of € 40.000, no wage and the taxpayer is at state pension age.

Step 1: profit = € 40.000.
Step 2: self-employed person's deduction at state pension age = € 2.515. Profit after the entrepreneur's allowance = € 40.000 minus € 2.515 = € 37.485.
Step 3: SME profit exemption = 14% x € 37.485 = € 5.247,90.
Step 4: taxable profit = € 37.485 minus € 5.247,90 = € 32.237,10.
Step 5: € 32.237,10 is below the maximum of € 66.956, so the contribution income is € 32.237,10.
Step 6: contribution = € 32.237,10 x 5,43% = € 1.750,47.
Note: the checked sources state no separate AOW-age rate or AOW-age exemption for the Zvw contribution itself, see section 7.

### Example 6. Calculated example, a private limited company (BV) pays the employer health insurance (zorgverzekering) contribution (werkgeversheffing Zvw) over the wage of a director and substantial shareholder (DGA, directeur-grootaandeelhouder)

Facts: a private limited company pays director and substantial shareholder (DGA) wage of € 50.000 in 2023 and the DGA is insured for the employee insurance schemes (werknemersverzekeringen).

Step 1: the employer health insurance contribution rate is 6,68% (section 2).
Step 2: € 50.000 x 6,68% = € 3.340 owed by the BV.
Step 3: contrast with the assessment (aanslag) rate. The same € 50.000 as contribution income by assessment would bear 5,43%, which is € 50.000 x 5,43% = € 2.715.
Step 4: € 50.000 is below the maximum of € 66.956 in both cases, so no cap applies.
