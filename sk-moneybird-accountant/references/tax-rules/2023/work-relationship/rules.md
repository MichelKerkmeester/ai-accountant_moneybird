## 2. HOW IT WORKS IN 2023

<a id="3-how-it-works"></a>

### 3.1 Wet DBA

The Deregulation of Employment Relationships Assessment Act (Wet deregulering beoordeling arbeidsrelaties, Wet DBA) is the Act of 3 February 2016 and entered into force on 1 May 2016. It replaced the prior work-relationship declaration (Verklaring arbeidsrelatie, VAR) (https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file). In 2023 this law was the framework for the question whether work is done in employment (loondienst) or by a self-employed person (zelfstandige). The law gives no fixed checklist. Qualification follows from the facts weighed by the courts (see 3.2) (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst). One common mix-up: Staatsblad 2016, 165 is not the Wet DBA itself. It is the Decree of 8 April 2016 that lets parties exclude the deemed employment relationship (fictieve dienstbetrekking) for home workers (thuiswerkers), persons treated as employees (gelijkgestelden) and artists (artiesten) by agreement, see 3.4 (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2016-165.html). Working outside employment does not by itself make the contractor (opdrachtnemer) an entrepreneur (ondernemer) for income tax or VAT (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst).

### 3.2 Employment test

Employment has three elements: the possibility of employer authority (werkgeversgezag), the duty to perform personal work (persoonlijke arbeid), and pay for the work (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst). The courts weigh factors including the nature and duration of the work, how work and working hours are set, embedding in the organisation, the duty to work personally, how the agreements were made, how pay is set and paid and its level, commercial risk and how far the contractor acts or can act as an entrepreneur (same URL). Not being in employment does not make someone an entrepreneur for income tax or VAT (same URL). So the employment relationship (arbeidsrelatie) answer and the fiscal entrepreneurship (ondernemerschap) answer are two separate questions.

### 3.3 Deemed employment relationships (fictieve dienstbetrekkingen)

Employment can also follow from a deemed employment relationship (fictieve dienstbetrekking) even where the ordinary test points the other way. The employment page lists contractors for work (aannemers van werk) and helpers, agents, artists and professional athletes (beroepssporters), persons treated as employees, interns (stagiairs), home workers and contractors working through an intermediary (intermediair) (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst). The persons treated as employees category is often relevant for a ZZP with one large client. It applies when the person usually works 2 or more days a week for the same client and earns at least 2/5 of the minimum wage (same URL). Both parts of that test must be met for the category to fit (same URL).

### 3.4 Opt-out for home workers, persons treated as employees and artists

Home workers, persons treated as employees and artists can be excluded from the deemed employment relationship by agreement. The exclusion must be in writing in the contract and must be made before the first wage payment. The contractor then has no entitlement to ZW, WIA and WW (sickness, disability and unemployment benefits) (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst). The legal basis is the Decree of 8 April 2016, Stb. 2016, 165 (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2016-165.html). The timing condition is strict. A clause added after the first wage payment does not meet the stated condition (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst). For the fiscal side, using this opt-out does not make the contractor an entrepreneur for income tax or VAT (same URL).

### 3.5 DBA enforcement regime 2023

Until 1 January 2025 the enforcement moratorium (handhavingsmoratorium) applied (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving). In a case without bad faith (non-kwaadwillend), the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) first gave an instruction (aanwijzing) instead of correcting at once. The instruction asks the parties to reshape the relationship so the contractor really works as a self-employed person, or to process it as an employment relationship in the payroll tax return (loonaangifte), usually within 3 months. If the instruction is not followed, a correction obligation (correctieverplichting) applies from the date of the instruction (same URL). Acting in bad faith means deliberately creating or continuing a situation of evident false self-employment (schijnzelfstandigheid), knowing or able to know that there is in fact an employment relationship. Cases of acting in bad faith could get correction obligations (correctieverplichtingen), supplementary payroll tax assessments (naheffingsaanslagen loonheffingen) and penalties (boetes) (same URL). In 2023 the practical route was therefore: first an instruction with a term, then corrections running from the instruction date if nothing changed. An enforcement plan (Handhavingsplan) was made for 2023 per the 2026 plan (same URL and https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z62fd.pdf).

### 3.6 End of the moratorium

Since 1 January 2025 the normal rules apply. No instructions (aanwijzingen) are given. False self-employment can lead directly to correction obligations and supplementary payroll tax assessments. Corrections go no further back than 1 January 2025 unless there is bad faith (kwaadwillendheid) or an instruction given before that date was not followed. In that case up to 5 years back. Only in 2030 can the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration again go back the full 5 years (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving). For a 2023 relationship this means a later correction reaches back to 1 January 2025 at the earliest, or further back in a case of acting in bad faith or when an instruction from before 2025 was not followed. The 2026 plan records that the ambition was 1 January 2026, that a separate plan was made again for 2026, and that the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration aims to integrate from 2027 (https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z62fd.pdf). The tranche 2025 plan had stated integration from 1 January 2026 (https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z52fd.pdf). This is enforcement policy, not statute. The penalty policy (boetebeleid) by year: 2025 no default penalties (verzuimboetes) and no intent penalties (vergrijpboetes) for the qualification of employment relationships (arbeidsrelaties) (Flach/Aartsen motion, Kamerstuk 31 311, nr. 266), and for corrections over 2025 this stays so. 2026 no default penalties, intent penalties possible under the normal rules from 1 January 2026, also for corrections over 2026. 2027 the remaining parts of the soft landing (zachte landing, no default penalties and in principle starting with a business visit (bedrijfsbezoek)) lapse on 1 January 2027 (https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z62fd.pdf and https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving). All of this is later than tax year 2023.

### 3.7 Case law on intention of the parties (partijbedoeling)

HR 6 November 2020, ECLI:NL:HR:2020:1746, deals with the role of intention of the parties in qualifying the work relationship (https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file). This ruling was part of the framework in 2023.

### 3.8 Deliveroo ruling

HR 24 March 2023, ECLI:NL:HR:2023:443 (Deliveroo), held that the meal delivery workers (maaltijdbezorgers) are employees (werknemers). The ruling contains a catalogue of circumstances (omstandighedencatalogus) that names integration (inbedding) and entrepreneurship as elements to weigh, and free substitution (vrije vervanging) is no absolute element but part of the catalogue (https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file). This is settled case law. The status of unknown in the earlier research is corrected to Corrected in the figures table. The ruling led to the withdrawal of the approval of the model agreements for free substitution (modelovereenkomsten vrije vervanging) on 14 August 2023, see 3.9 (same URL). A later development, not part of tax year 2023: a ruling on platform work (Uber) holds that there is no order of priority (rangorde) between the factors (gezichtspunten), so entrepreneurship is a full criterion, and the government states this has no effect on enforcement (handhaving) (https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2025/03/27/ondernemerschap-blijft-volwaardig-criterium-bij-beoordelen-schijnzelfstandigheid). The exact date and ECLI of that later ruling are UNVERIFIED, see section 7.

### 3.9 Model agreements for free substitution

Working under an approved model agreement (modelovereenkomst) means no employment, but only if the parties work as described in it (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/werken-met-modelovereenkomsten). The approval of the model agreements for free substitution was withdrawn on 14 August 2023 after the Deliveroo ruling (https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file). From that date those specific model agreements no longer carry the approval. This is the 2023 event in this topic. The general stop on new model agreements came later, see 3.10.

### 3.10 Model agreements stop and use-up-to date

Since 6 September 2024 no new model agreements are assessed and none are renewed. Approved agreements that were valid on 6 September 2024 can be used up to and including 31 December 2029, even if the agreement states another validity date (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/geen-nieuwe-modelovereenkomsten-meer). The reason given is that model agreements can create false certainty (schijnzekerheid), because the qualification depends on how the work is actually done (same URL). The tranche 2025 plan adds that they are honoured to end 2029 after the Van Oostenbruggen motion (motie Van Oostenbruggen) (Kamerstuk 31 311, nr. 275), on the condition that they comply with law and regulations (https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z52fd.pdf). For tax year 2023 this whole item is later than the year. The condition that is relevant to 2023 work stays the same: the model agreement protects only if the parties work exactly as described (https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/werken-met-modelovereenkomsten).

### 3.11 Web module for assessing employment relationships (Webmodule Beoordeling Arbeidsrelatie)

The web module for assessing employment relationships is meant for clients (opdrachtgevers) with a business in the Netherlands. It has three outcomes: an indication that the work can be done outside an employment relationship, an indication of an employment relationship, or no judgment possible (https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/personeel/inhuren/webmodule-beoordeling-arbeidsrelatie/). It is not a legal decision and gives no rights. It is not useful for a contract with a legal entity (rechtspersoon) such as a bv, for a broker (bemiddelaar) or intermediary (tussenpersoon), or for a foreign contractor (same URL). The pilot ran from 11 January 2021 for 6 months and ended on 10 July 2021, the online pilot environment stayed active, and there was no legal force (rechtskracht) during or after the pilot (https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file). The pilot design used a points system with a client declaration (opdrachtgeversverklaring) as one outcome (same URL). In 2023 the tool existed, and its outcome did not bind the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration.

### 3.13 VBAR rule

The enacted rule is new art. 7:610aa BW. Subsection 1: someone who works for another for pay of at most the hourly amount is presumed to work under an employment contract (arbeidsovereenkomst). Subsection 2: the Minister of SZW changes the amount in the manner of art. 14 lid 1 and 2 of the Minimum Wage and Minimum Holiday Allowance Act (Wet minimumloon en minimumvakantiebijslag), rounded up to whole euros, always starting from the unrounded amount. Subsection 3: the presumption does not apply when the other party is a natural person not acting in a profession or business. Article II provides an evaluation within 5 years. Article III, subsection 2, says that for the first application after entry into force the applicable amount is given by ministerial regulation (ministeriële regeling) (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html). The effect is that the worker (werkende) can invoke the presumption and the client (opdrachtgever) must prove that there is no employment contract (https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/wetswijzigingen/laag-uurtarief-van-zzper-kan-leiden-tot-arbeidsovereenkomst/). It is civil law only, with no fiscal qualification rule (same URL). In 2023 this was proposed law at most and not in force.

### 3.14 VBAR hourly threshold, statutory text

The explanatory memorandum (memorie van toelichting) explains the € 36 in the bill text. The build-up at the reference date (peildatum) 1 January 2025 is: 120% of the statutory minimum wage (wettelijk minimumloon, WML) of € 14,06 per hour, plus a 25% surcharge (opslag) for disability insurance for the self-employed (arbeidsongeschiktheidsverzekering, AOV) and pension and a 15% surcharge for general costs, times 1,5 for non-billable hours. That gives an unrounded € 35,43 excluding VAT (excl. btw) at the reference date 1 January 2025, rounded up to € 36,00. Every later change starts from the unrounded € 35,43 and follows the WML percentage (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html). The WML used for 1 January 2025 is € 14,06 per hour (https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2025). Article III, subsection 2, provides that the amount for the first application after entry into force is given by ministerial regulation. That regulation (regeling) was not found on 2026-10-11, see section 7 (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html).

### 3.15 VBAR hourly threshold, indexed

The WML rose to € 14,71 per hour on 1 January 2026 (https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2026). Under the subsection 2 mechanism every later change starts from the unrounded € 35,43 and follows the WML percentage, so the unrounded amount at reference date 1 January 2026 is about € 37,07 and the rounded amount is € 38 (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html and https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36783_wet_invoering). This € 38 (reference date 1 January 2026) is what the Senate, the Government of the Netherlands (rijksoverheid.nl) and the government business portal (Ondernemersplein) state (https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36783_wet_invoering, https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/03/06/kabinet-kiest-voor-meer-rust-en-duidelijkheid-voor-zzpers-en-opdrachtgevers, https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/wetswijzigingen/laag-uurtarief-van-zzper-kan-leiden-tot-arbeidsovereenkomst/). So € 36 is the statutory base text at reference date 2025 and € 38 is the indexed amount at reference date 2026. In tax year 2023 neither amount was in force.

### 3.16 Interaction guidelines (richtlijnen doorwerking) for income tax (IH) and VAT (OB)

The guidelines for the interaction with income tax (inkomstenbelasting) and VAT (omzetbelasting) for the contractor exist since 2023 and were updated for the end of the moratorium (https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z52fd.pdf). The focus is on the payroll taxes at the client. After LH corrections at the client the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration in principle takes no targeted action for earlier years at the contractor aimed at source qualification (bronkwalificatie) (the qualification of the income source). Earlier years means tax years before the year of the LH correction. A contractor under regular income tax (IH) or VAT (OB) supervision has the most recent open return ((winst)aangifte) or periods (tijdvakken) examined, with attention to future returns (same PDF and https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handhavingsplan-arbeidsrelaties-lh0021z62fd.pdf).

### 3.17 BAZ, bill 36 912

The BAZ appears as a separate bill, 36 912, proposed. It was submitted on 24 March 2026, a technical briefing (technische briefing) followed on 13 May 2026, the contribution to the debate on the report (inbreng verslag) was delivered on 27 May 2026 after a postponement from 20 May, and the report (36 912, nr. 5) was published on 29 May 2026. On 2026-10-11 it was awaiting the memorandum in response to the report (nota naar aanleiding van het verslag), with no debate and no vote. The docket summary gives about 70% of income up to the minimum wage, a waiting period of about one year, and an opt-out with a qualifying private policy (https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/wetsvoorstellen/detail?cfg=wetsvoorsteldetails&qry=wetsvoorstel%3A36912). The bill is later than tax year 2023 and changes nothing in the 2023 rules. It is included here because the same discussion about self-employment (zelfstandigheid) names it.

The income tax return for 2023 must arrive before 1 May 2024 (https://web.archive.org/web/20240413/https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/prive/inkomstenbelasting/hoe_werkt_inkomstenbelasting/aanslag/definitieve_aanslag).

### 3.19 Income tax return 2023 with deferral (uitstel)

A granted deferral (uitstel) gives until before 1 September 2024 (https://web.archive.org/web/20240413/https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/prive/inkomstenbelasting/hoe_werkt_inkomstenbelasting/aanslag/definitieve_aanslag).

### 3.20 Final assessment (definitieve aanslag) 2023 latest

The final assessment for 2023 follows no later than 31 December 2026 when the return is filed before 1 May 2024, and no later than 30 April 2027 with 4 months deferral (https://web.archive.org/web/20240413/https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/prive/inkomstenbelasting/hoe_werkt_inkomstenbelasting/aanslag/definitieve_aanslag).
