## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### VAT rates on domestic sales

**Calculated example.** A sale of goods subject to the standard rate has a net price of € 1.000.

1. Net amount: € 1.000.
2. VAT at 21%: € 1.000 × 21% = € 210.
3. Invoice total: € 1.000 + € 210 = € 1.210.

For a qualifying 9% supply with the same net amount, VAT is € 1.000 × 9% = € 90, and the invoice total is € 1.000 + € 90 = € 1.090. For a qualifying zero-rated supply, VAT is € 1.000 × 0% = € 0. The applicable rates and conditions are set out in section 2. [Wet OB, 1 January 2024](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0002629/2024-01-01)

### Exempt versus zero-rated purchase

**Calculated example.** A business buys an input used only for a taxable zero-rated supply. The input price is € 1.000 and its invoice shows € 210 input VAT (voorbelasting) at 21%.

1. Output VAT on the zero-rated sale is calculated at 0%.
2. Input VAT attributable to that zero-rated supply remains deductible, subject to the deduction conditions.
3. If the same input is used only for an exempt supply, the € 210 is not deductible (niet aftrekbaar).

This example illustrates the difference between a zero rate and an exemption. [Belastingdienst handbook](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handboek_ondernemen_2026_aa9701z62fd.pdf)

### KOR overrun

**Official example.** Turnover is € 19.750 on 15 September 2024. A € 500 advance received the next day raises 2024 turnover above € 20.000. The ordinary VAT rules apply from 16 September, and the entire € 500 advance is taxed. [Belastingdienst KOR opt-out guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/afmelden-kor)

### Solar panels

**Calculated example.** A home installation that qualifies for the zero rate has a net price of € 5.000.

1. Net amount: € 5.000.
2. VAT at 0%: € 5.000 × 0% = € 0.
3. Invoice total: € 5.000 + € 0 = € 5.000.

The example assumes the supply and installation meet the covered conditions. It does not apply to items or installations subject to 21%. [Decision of 16 June 2023](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2023-17463.html)

### Private-use car

**Calculated example.** Assume a business car has a catalogue price (catalogusprijs) of € 30.000 including VAT and BPM. No private-use records are available, and the 2,7% rate applies.

1. € 30.000 × 2,7% = € 810.
2. Compare the result with the applicable cap, which is based on VAT deducted in the year and the relevant purchase VAT.
3. Report no more than the applicable cap in the last return of the year.

The example does not determine the cap because the amount of VAT deducted and the car’s use history are not specified. [Decision on VAT for private car use](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0043783/2020-07-04)

### BUA gifts and staff provisions

**Official example.** A gift costs € 220, an employee contributes € 100, and a Christmas package costs € 80. Input VAT deducted on the combined € 300 is € 63. VAT due on the € 100 contribution is € 21. The remaining repayment is € 63 − € 21 = € 42. For 2024, compare the total cost per recipient with the € 227 BUA threshold without subtracting the contribution. [Belastingdienst BUA threshold and own-contribution guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/btw_aftrekken/personeelsvoorzieningen_en_relatiegeschenken/drempelbedrag_personeelsvoorzieningen_giften_en_relatiegeschenken), [BUA decision](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2023-28124.html)

### Invoice deadline

**Official example.** If the supply is delivered on 10 April, the invoice is due by 15 May. The invoice should state the amount excluding VAT, the applicable rate and the VAT amount. [Belastingdienst handbook 2020](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handboek_ondernemen_2020_aa9701z01fd.pdf), [Belastingdienst invoice requirements](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/administratie_bijhouden/facturen_maken/factuureisen/factuureisen)

### Reverse charge

**Calculated example.** A domestic reverse-charged service has a net amount of € 1.000 and is subject to 21%.

1. Supplier invoices € 1.000 without VAT and marks the invoice “btw verlegd”.
2. Customer calculates VAT: € 1.000 × 21% = € 210.
3. Customer reports the € 210 as reverse-charged VAT (verlegde btw).
4. If the service is used for taxable activity and ordinary deduction conditions are met, the customer may deduct the same € 210.

The supplier must still apply the reverse-charge rule correctly. [Belastingdienst reverse-charge guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/btw_berekenen_aan_uw_klanten/waarover_btw_berekenen/verleggingsregeling/hoe_werkt_btw_verleggen)

### EU distance sales

**Calculated example.** A supplier established in one member state has qualifying distance sales and digital services of € 9.000 in the current calendar year and then makes another qualifying sale of € 2.000.

1. The first amount is € 9.000.
2. Add the next sale: € 9.000 + € 2.000 = € 11.000.
3. The total is above the € 10.000 threshold.
4. The foreign VAT rule applies from the transaction that crosses the threshold.

This assumes the previous-year test and the other threshold conditions are met. [Belastingdienst distance-sales guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/btw-goederen-eu-particulieren)
