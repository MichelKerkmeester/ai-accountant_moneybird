### Example 1: instruction (aanwijzing) route for a client acting in good faith (non-kwaadwillende opdrachtgever)

This example uses only the rules in section 3.1 and 3.2. Inputs are invented.

- A client has one worker who invoices through a one-person business. The relationship in fact shows employer authority (werkgeversgezag), personal work and pay, so it is an employment relationship.
- The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) finds this during a check. The case is not acting in bad faith (kwaadwillend).
- Step 1: the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration gives an instruction. The usual term is 3 months.
- Step 2: the client does nothing within the term.
- Step 3: a correction obligation (correctieverplichting) applies from the date of the instruction, not from the start of the relationship.
- Step 4: if the client had reshaped the relationship or processed it in the payroll tax return (loonaangifte) within the term, no correction obligation would have followed from the instruction route.

Arithmetic: no amount is computed here because the material gives no rate or base for the correction. The step that matters is the date, the date of the instruction.

### Example 2: the two thresholds in the 2020 assessment (aanslag)

This example uses the section 3.15 figures. It reads the threshold (grens) in the wording that was current until the 2023 captures, equal to or more than (gelijk aan of meer dan), and prints the 2020 values € 48 and € 16 from that wording.

- Input: the final income tax calculation over 2020 comes out at a payable amount of exactly € 47.
- Under the older wording, € 47 is below equal to or more than € 48, so no assessment (aanslag) is imposed.
- Input: the calculation comes out at exactly € 48.
- Under the older wording, € 48 is equal to or more than € 48, so an assessment is imposed.
- Input: the calculation comes out as a refund of exactly € 15.
- Under the older wording, € 15 is below equal to or more than € 16, so no refund is paid.
- Input: the calculation comes out as a refund of exactly € 16.
- Under the older wording, a refund of € 16 is equal to or more than € 16, so the refund is paid.

Cross-check with the current wording: pay € 47 is not higher than € 47, so no assessment, and € 48 is higher than € 47, so an assessment. Refund € 15 is not higher than € 15, so no refund, and € 16 is higher than € 15, so a refund. For whole euros the two wordings give the same result.

### Example 4: tax interest (belastingrente) on a late 2020 return (Calculated example)

This example uses the section 3.8, 3.10 and 3.12 figures. Inputs are invented.

- The return for 2020 is filed on 20 May 2021, after the 8 May 2021 deadline. No deferral was granted.
- Tax interest runs from 1 July 2021 at 4% per year.
- Day count: 30 days per month, 360 days per year.
- Step 1: from 1 July 2021 to 31 December 2021 is 6 months, so 6 x 30 = 180 days.
- Step 2: from 1 January 2022 to 30 June 2022 is 6 months, so 6 x 30 = 180 days.
- Step 3: total period used in this example is 360 days, which is one 360-day year.
- Step 4: tax interest before rounding: 4% x 360 / 360 = 4% of the tax amount.
- Step 5: on an open amount of € 2.000 the tax interest is € 2.000 x 4% = € 80.
- Step 6: rounded down to whole euros: € 80.

The actual end date of the interest period is 6 weeks after the assessment date, and the example fixes the period only to show the arithmetic.

### Example 5: a late VAT (btw) return in 2020 (Calculated example)

This example uses the section 3.21 figure. Inputs are invented.

- A monthly filer sends the May 2020 return after the 30 June 2020 date.
- The leniency (coulance) is 7 calendar days (kalenderdagen).
- Step 1: the return is 20 days late, which is more than 7 days.
- Step 2: the leniency does not cover it.
- Step 3: the default fine (verzuimboete) is € 68 for 2020.
- Interaction: tax interest on VAT (btw) runs from 1 January after the tax year, so for a May 2020 period the tax interest start is 1 January 2021. A voluntary correction within 3 months after the tax year avoids tax interest.

### Example 6: the hourly amount method behind the later legal presumption (rechtsvermoeden)

This example uses the section 3.26 figures to show the method. It is not 2020 law and no 2020 rule uses it.

- Step 1: take 120% of the WML of € 14,06 per hour at 1 January 2025: 1,20 x € 14,06 = € 16,872.
- Step 2: add 25% surcharge (opslag) for AOV and pension and 15% surcharge for general costs, so multiply by 1,40: € 16,872 x 1,40 = € 23,6208.
- Step 3: multiply by 1,5 for non-billable hours: € 23,6208 x 1,5 = € 35,4312.
- Step 4: round up to whole euros: € 36,00. This is the statutory base amount at reference date (peildatum) 1 January 2025.
- Step 5: indexation follows the WML percentage. The WML rose from € 14,06 to € 14,71 on 1 January 2026. The ratio is € 14,71 / € 14,06 = 1,04623, or about 4,62%.
- Step 6: apply the ratio to the unrounded € 35,43: € 35,43 x 1,04623 = about € 37,07.
- Step 7: round up to whole euros: € 38. This is the amount printed on primary pages for reference date 1 January 2026.
