## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### Calculated example: Vpb in the two brackets

Input: a BV has a taxable amount of € 500.000 in 2021.

Step 1: the first bracket up to and including € 245.000 at 15% gives € 245.000 × 0,15 = € 36.750.

Step 2: the part above the bracket is € 500.000 - € 245.000 = € 255.000.

Step 3: 25% of € 255.000 is € 255.000 × 0,25 = € 63.750.

Step 4: total Vpb is € 36.750 + € 63.750 = € 100.500.

Check: the art. 22 column III amount is € 36.750, so the outcome matches the statutory series in section 2.

### Calculated example: book year straddling 1 January 2022

Input: a book year runs from 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2022 with a taxable amount of € 300.000. Art. 22 changes on 1 January 2022, so art. 31 applies.

Step 1: B is 365 days, X is 184 days (July to December 2021) and Y is 181 days (January to June 2022). 184 + 181 = 365.

Step 2: To under the 2021 art. 22 is € 245.000 × 0,15 = € 36.750, plus 25% × (€ 300.000 - € 245.000) = 25% × € 55.000 = € 13.750, so To = € 36.750 + € 13.750 = € 50.500.

Step 3: Tn under the 2022 art. 22 is € 300.000 × 0,15 = € 45.000, because € 300.000 is below € 395.000.

Step 4: tax = (184/365 × € 50.500) + (181/365 × € 45.000) = € 25.457,53 + € 22.315,07 = € 47.772,60.

### Calculated example: generic interest deduction limitation (generieke renteaftrekbeperking)

Input: a BV has a net interest expense of € 2.000.000 and an adjusted profit (gecorrigeerde winst) of € 5.000.000 in 2021.

Step 1: 30% of € 5.000.000 = € 1.500.000.

Step 2: the higher of € 1.500.000 and € 1.000.000 is € 1.500.000.

Step 3: the net interest expense exceeds € 1.500.000 by € 2.000.000 - € 1.500.000 = € 500.000.

So € 1.500.000 is deductible in 2021 and € 500.000 is not.

### Calculated example: loss offsetting (verliesverrekening)

Input: a BV has a loss of € 80.000 in 2021 and a profit of € 60.000 in 2020.

Step 1: one year back, € 60.000 of the 2021 loss is set off against the 2020 profit.

Step 2: the remaining loss is € 80.000 - € 60.000 = € 20.000. It carries forward to the six following years, 2022 to 2027.

Step 3: the inspector fixes the loss by decision, and set-off follows the order in which losses arose.

Check: the € 20.000 is below the 2022 per-year cap of € 1.000.000 plus 50% of the profit above € 1.000.000, so the cap does not limit this loss.

### Calculated example: innovation box (innovatiebox) fraction, flat rate (forfait) and nexus

Input: a qualifying advantage of € 100.000 in 2021. H is the highest art. 22 rate, 25%.

Step 1: the fraction is 9/25 = 0,36.

Step 2: the amount that enters the taxable profit is 0,36 × € 100.000 = € 36.000.

Step 3: at the top rate of 25%, the tax is € 36.000 × 0,25 = € 9.000, which is 9% of the € 100.000 advantage.

Flat rate check: a profit before art. 12b of € 80.000 gives 25% × € 80.000 = € 20.000, below the € 25.000 maximum. A profit before art. 12b of € 160.000 gives 25% × € 160.000 = € 40.000, capped at € 25.000.

Nexus check: a qualifying expenditure of € 100.000 gives K = € 100.000 × 1,3 = € 130.000. The K/T part is capped at the whole advantage after the art. 12bc threshold (drempel). No amount for that threshold is stated in the sources used here.

### Calculated example: participation exemption (deelnemingsvrijstelling)

Input: a BV holds 5% of the nominal paid-up capital of another BV and receives a dividend of € 40.000.

Art. 13 lid 1 leaves the advantage out of the profit, so no Vpb is due on the € 40.000. A holding below 5% is not a participation (deelneming) for art. 13 lid 2.

### Calculated example: fiscal unity (fiscale eenheid)

Input: a parent company (BV) holds 95% of a subsidiary (BV). The subsidiary has a profit of € 200.000 and the parent company has a loss of € 50.000.

Step 1: the combined taxable amount is € 200.000 - € 50.000 = € 150.000.

Step 2: Vpb is € 150.000 × 0,15 = € 22.500, because the amount is below € 245.000.

Check: without the fiscal unity the subsidiary would pay € 200.000 × 0,15 = € 30.000 and the parent company's loss would stand on its own.

### Calculated example: customary salary (gebruikelijk loon)

Input: a DGA works for his BV. The wage from the most comparable employment relationship is € 80.000. The highest wage of the employees of the body and connected bodies is € 55.000. The standard amount (normbedrag) is € 47.000.

Step 1: 75% of € 80.000 = € 60.000.

Step 2: the highest of € 60.000, € 55.000 and € 47.000 is € 60.000. The wage must be at least € 60.000.

Escape check: if the highest amount were € 70.000 and the BV makes plausible that this exceeds 75% of the comparable wage, the wage is 75% of the comparable wage, € 60.000, but at least the standard amount of € 47.000. The outcome stays € 60.000.

Floor check: if the wage for all work in the body and connected bodies is € 4.500, the rule does not apply.

### Calculated example: start-up relief (startersfaciliteit)

Input: a starter in 2021 with an S&O-verklaring to which the start-up percentage applies.

The statutory minimum is 108% × 12 × the art. 8 lid 1 sub a WML amount. No 2021 WML amount is stated in the sources used here, so no euro outcome is computed. The check is against the lower comparable wage if made plausible, within the de-minimis ceiling of Regulation (verordening) (EU) nr. 1407/2013. A first use in 2021 can run at most 3 years.

### Calculated example: Zvw for a DGA

Input: an uninsured DGA with a wage of € 58.311 in 2021.

Step 1: the maximum contribution income (bijdrage-inkomen) is € 58.311.

Step 2: the contribution is 5,75% × € 58.311 = € 3.352,88.

Step 3: a wage of € 80.000 gives the same outcome, because only € 58.311 counts.

Input: an insured DGA with a wage of € 58.311 in 2021. The employer levy (werkgeversheffing) is 7,00% × € 58.311 = € 4.081,77.

### Calculated example: box 2 and dividend tax (dividendbelasting)

Input: an individual receives a dividend of € 60.000 from a BV in 2021.

Step 1: box 2 tax at 26,9% is € 60.000 × 0,269 = € 16.140.

Step 2: dividend tax withheld at 15% is € 60.000 × 0,15 = € 9.000.

Step 3: the credit in the income tax return leaves € 16.140 - € 9.000 = € 7.140.

### Calculated example: dividend tax and the dividend statement (dividendnota)

Input: a BV pays proceeds (opbrengst) of € 60.000 to a Dutch shareholder who is not exempt.

Step 1: dividend tax of 15% × € 60.000 = € 9.000 is withheld.

Step 2: the shareholder receives € 60.000 - € 9.000 = € 51.000.

Step 3: a dividend statement is due and the return and payment are due within 1 month after the day the dividend is made available.

### Calculated example: grossing up (brutering)

Input: proceeds of € 85.000 where the company bears the dividend tax.

Step 1: art. 6 lid 1 multiplies the proceeds by 100/85: € 85.000 × 100/85 = € 100.000.

Step 2: dividend tax is 15% × € 100.000 = € 15.000.

Step 3: the shareholder receives € 85.000.

### Calculated example: conditional withholding tax (conditionele bronbelasting)

Input: a BV pays € 200.000 of royalties to a related body in a low-tax jurisdiction in 2021.

Step 1: interest and royalties are in scope in the 2021 version, dividends are not.

Step 2: the rate is the highest art. 22 rate, 25% in 2021.

Step 3: the tax is € 200.000 × 0,25 = € 50.000.

### Calculated example: annual accounts (jaarrekening) size classes

Input: a BV meets these criteria on two consecutive balance dates: assets € 400.000, net turnover € 800.000, 8 employees.

Micro: assets € 400.000 is above € 350.000 (fail), turnover € 800.000 is above € 700.000 (fail), 8 employees is fewer than 10 (meet). Only one of three, so not micro.

Small: € 400.000 is at most € 6 million (meet), € 800.000 is at most € 12 million (meet), 8 is fewer than 50 (meet). Three of three, so small.

Second input: assets € 6.500.000, net turnover € 13.000.000, 40 employees.

Small: assets fail, turnover fails and 40 employees meets the fewer than 50 test. Only one of three, so not small.

Medium: assets are at most € 20 million (meet), turnover is at most € 40 million (meet) and 40 employees is fewer than 250 (meet). Three of three, so medium.

### Calculated example: discontinuation deduction (stakingsaftrek)

Input: a taxable contribution (ruisende inbreng) with a profit from business discontinuation (stakingswinst) of € 50.000.

Step 1: the discontinuation deduction is at most € 3.630.

Step 2: the taxable profit from business discontinuation is € 50.000 - € 3.630 = € 46.370.

Check: the discontinuation deduction is once in a lifetime, and an extra annuity (lijfrente) premium deduction applies. Hidden reserves realised by a direct withdrawal (onttrekking) before a tax-neutral conversion (geruisloze omzetting) do not get the discontinuation deduction but may go into an annuity.
