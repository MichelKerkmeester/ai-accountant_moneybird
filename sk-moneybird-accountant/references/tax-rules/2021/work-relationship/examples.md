## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

The checked sources give no official examples for this topic for 2021. Every example below is a Calculated example built here from the section 2 figures, with clearly invented facts. No real person's data is used.

### Example 1: Employment (loondienst) test on invented facts (Calculated example)

Facts (invented): from 1 February 2021 a graphic designer works 4 days a week for one client for 8 months. The client sets the working hours and the place of work. The designer uses the client's equipment and email address and works in the client's team. The contract says the work must be done personally. The fee is a fixed monthly amount. No commercial risk sits with the designer.

Step 1: check the three elements from section 2. Employer authority (werkgeversgezag): the client sets work and working hours, so the possibility of authority is present. Personal work (persoonlijke arbeid): the contract requires the designer to do the work personally. Wage: there is a fixed monthly fee. All three elements are present.

Step 2: weigh the section 2 factors. Embedding in the organisation is high. The duty to work personally is present. The fee is a fixed monthly amount and is not linked to a result. Commercial risk is low. The designer has one client and no own equipment, so the designer does not act as an entrepreneur.

Step 3: factors that could point the other way: the duration is 8 months and the parties chose the label contract for services (overeenkomst van opdracht). The label is part of how the agreements were made, which is one of the weighing factors in section 2. On these facts the embedding and the set hours and place of work point to employment.

Step 4: a conclusion of no employment would still not make the designer an entrepreneur for income tax (inkomstenbelasting) or VAT. That is a separate test.

Sources: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst and https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file.

### Example 2: Written opt-out for a person treated as an employee (gelijkgestelde)

Facts (invented): a home worker (thuiswerker) works for one client 3 days a week for 6 months. The pay is above 2/5 of the minimum wage (minimumloon).

Step 1: the section 2 condition for persons treated as employees (gelijkgestelden) is usually 2 or more days a week for the same client. With 3 days a week the first part of the condition is met. The 2/5 minimum wage part cannot be checked in euros here, because the checked sources give no 2021 minimum wage.

Step 2: if the persons treated as employees rule applies, the parties can exclude the deemed employment relationship (fictieve dienstbetrekking) in writing in the contract before the first payment of pay.

Step 3: the effect is that the contractor (opdrachtnemer) has no entitlement to ZW, WIA and WW.

Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst.

### Example 3: Instruction (aanwijzing) term of usually 3 months (Calculated example)

Facts (invented): a client (opdrachtgever) receives an instruction dated 1 March 2021.

Step 1: the section 2 term is usually 3 months.

Step 2: 1 March 2021 plus 3 months is 1 June 2021.

Step 3: by that date the client must reshape the relationship so the contractor really works as a self-employed person (zelfstandige), or process it as an employment relationship in the payroll tax return (loonaangifte).

Step 4: if the instruction is not followed, the correction obligation (correctieverplichting) applies from the date of the instruction, so from 1 March 2021.

Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.

### Example 4: Model agreement (modelovereenkomst) not followed in practice (Calculated example)

Facts (invented): the parties use an approved model agreement. In practice the contractor works at the client's office during hours set by the client and takes instructions from the client's team lead, so the work is not done as the model agreement describes.

Step 1: the section 2 rule is no employment only if the parties work as described.

Step 2: here they do not, so the model agreement does not give the no-employment position.

Step 3: the qualification then follows how the work is actually done, and on these facts the set hours, the set place of work and the instructions point to employment.

Step 4: the records should show the actual way of working, because the qualification depends on how the work is actually done.

Sources: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/werken-met-modelovereenkomsten and https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst.

### Example 5: Web module (webmodule) not useful for a contract with a BV (Calculated example)

Facts (invented): a client wants to check a contract with a contractor that is a BV, a legal entity (rechtspersoon).

Step 1: the web module is for clients (opdrachtgevers) with a business in the Netherlands.

Step 2: the section 2 limit says it is not useful for a contract with a legal entity such as a BV.

Step 3: even in a useful case the outcome is not a legal decision and gives no rights, so it cannot be used as the qualification.

Source: https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/personeel/inhuren/webmodule-beoordeling-arbeidsrelatie/.

### Example 6: Intention of the parties (partijbedoeling) weighed with the other factors (Calculated example)

Facts (invented): the parties call the contract a contract for services and state that no employment relationship is intended. In practice the client sets the hours and the place of work and the work is embedded in the client's team.

Step 1: HR 6 November 2020, ECLI:NL:HR:2020:1746, concerns the role of intention of the parties.

Step 2: the label and the stated intention are part of how the agreements were made, which is one of the weighing factors in section 2.

Step 3: they are weighed with the other factors. The checked material gives the subject of the ruling, not its full holding, so this example uses the section 2 weighing factors only.

Step 4: on these facts the embedding, the set hours and the set place of work point to employment.

Source: https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d2c6b4e8-537c-48a8-9e17-85f1acf4825d/file.

### Example 7: VBAR build-up of € 36 and indexation to € 38 (Calculated example, not applicable to tax year 2021)

This example shows the later Act on Clarification of the Assessment of Employment Relationships and Legal Presumption (Wet verduidelijking beoordeling arbeidsrelaties en rechtsvermoeden, VBAR) method only. It does not apply to tax year 2021.

Step 1: 120% of the WML of € 14,06 = € 16,872.

Step 2: 25% of € 16,872 = € 4,218.

Step 3: 15% of € 16,872 = € 2,5308.

Step 4: € 16,872 + € 4,218 + € 2,5308 = € 23,6208.

Step 5: € 23,6208 times 1,5 = € 35,4312, the unrounded € 35,43 excluding VAT at reference date (peildatum) 1 January 2025.

Step 6: rounded up to whole euros = € 36.

Step 7: every later change starts from the unrounded € 35,43 and follows the WML percentage. The WML rose to € 14,71 on 1 January 2026. € 14,71 / € 14,06 = about 1,04623.

Step 8: € 35,43 times 1,04623 = about € 37,07 unrounded.

Step 9: rounded up to whole euros = € 38 at reference date 1 January 2026. The € 38 itself is stated on the primary pages, the step from the unrounded amount is calculated here.

Sources: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html and https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2026.

### Example 8: BAZ docket summary on invented facts (Calculated example, not applicable to tax year 2021)

This example shows the later Basic Disability Insurance for Self-Employed Persons Act (Wet basisverzekering arbeidsongeschiktheid zelfstandigen, BAZ) bill only. It does not apply to tax year 2021.

Facts (invented): a self-employed person with an invented profit of € 30.000 becomes unable to work.

Step 1: the section 2 docket summary gives about 70% of income up to the minimum wage. 70% of € 30.000 = € 21.000, but the basis is capped at the minimum wage and the checked sources give no minimum wage amount for the BAZ, so no benefit amount is computed here.

Step 2: the waiting period (wachttijd) is about one year.

Step 3: an opt-out is possible with a qualifying private policy.

Step 4: the bill was not law in 2021 and was awaiting the memorandum in response to the report (nota naar aanleiding van het verslag) on 2026-10-11.

Source: https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/wetsvoorstellen/detail?cfg=wetsvoorsteldetails&qry=wetsvoorstel%3A36912.
