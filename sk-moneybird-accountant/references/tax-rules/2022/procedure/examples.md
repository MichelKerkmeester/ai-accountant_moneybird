## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Final assessment (definitieve aanslag) 2022 timing (Official example)

The archived page gives the example itself. A return over 2022 filed before 1 May 2023 gives the final assessment at the latest on 31 December 2025. With 4 months deferral (uitstel) the return is due before 1 September 2023 and the final assessment at the latest on 30 April 2026 ([source](https://web.archive.org/web/20230401/https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/prive/inkomstenbelasting/hoe_werkt_inkomstenbelasting/aanslag/definitieve_aanslag)).

### Example 2: VAT tax interest (belastingrente) on 2022 quarters (Official example)

The official example: a 2022 quarterly return turns out to be wrong and a supplementary return (suppletie) is filed. A supplementary return before 1 April 2023 gives no tax interest. A supplementary return after 1 April 2023 gives interest (rente) over the period from 1 January 2023 to 14 days after the supplementary assessment (naheffingsaanslag) date ([source](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/standaard_functies/prive/contact/rechten_en_plichten_bij_de_belastingdienst/belastingrente/belastingrente_betalen_bij_loonbelasting_btw_en_overdrachtsbelasting)).

### Example 3: Additional assessment (navordering) example (Official example)

The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration example: tax year 2016, with 5 months deferral, an additional assessment is possible until 31 May 2022 ([source](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/standaard_functies/prive/contact/rechten_en_plichten_bij_de_belastingdienst/navorderingsaanslag)). The example anchors how the 5-year term and the deferral extension are counted.

### Example 4: Tax interest on income tax for tax year 2022 (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: a final income tax due of € 10.000 for 2022, a return filed on 20 April 2023 (so before 1 May 2023), and an assessment that does not follow the return, dated 1 November 2023.

Step 1: the interest period starts on 1 July 2023, the 1 July after the tax year.

Step 2: it ends 6 weeks after the assessment date. 6 weeks is 42 days. 1 November 2023 plus 42 days is 13 December 2023.

Step 3: the whole period falls in the 6% rate (1 July 2023 to 31 December 2023). The 7,5% for days in 2024 does not apply.

Step 4: with 30 days per month and 360 days per year, 1 July 2023 to 1 December 2023 is 5 months, that is 5 × 30 = 150 days. 1 December 2023 to 13 December 2023 is 12 days. Total 150 + 12 = 162 days.

Step 5: interest = € 10.000 × 6% × 162/360 = € 600 × 0,45 = € 270. Rounded down to whole euros: € 270.

Contrast: if the return had been filed before 1 May 2023 and the assessment had followed it unchanged, no tax interest would be charged.

### Example 5: Additional assessment period (navorderingstermijn) for tax year 2022 (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: tax year 2022, deferral of 4 months (return due before 1 September 2023), so the extension is 4 months.

Step 1: the 5-year term is counted from 1 January after the tax year, that is 1 January 2023.

Step 2: 5 years on gives 31 December 2027.

Step 3: extend by the deferral duration of 4 months. 31 December 2027 plus 4 months is 30 April 2028.

So an additional assessment over 2022 is possible until 30 April 2028, unless a longer term applies (12 years for foreign income or assets). The official example for 2016 with 5 months deferral runs to 31 May 2022, which follows the same counting.

### Example 6: Payment of a provisional assessment (voorlopige aanslag) dated in the year (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: a provisional assessment 2022 of € 3.600 dated 15 March 2022.

Step 1: months remaining after the month of the date (March 2022): April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December, that is 9 months.

Step 2: the amount is payable in as many equal monthly instalments (termijnen) as months remain, so 9 instalments.

Step 3: € 3.600 / 9 = € 400 per instalment.

Step 4: the first instalment is one month after the date, on 15 April 2022, and the following instalments run monthly to 15 December 2022.

For comparison, an assessment would be collectable 6 weeks after the date of the assessment notice (aanslagbiljet), and an additional assessment (navorderingsaanslag) 1 month after its date.

### Example 7: Objection (bezwaar) deadline (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: an assessment dated 15 October 2023.

Step 1: the objection period (bezwaartermijn) is 6 weeks after the date of the assessment.

Step 2: 6 weeks is 42 days.

Step 3: 15 October 2023 plus 42 days is 26 November 2023.

So the objection must be made by 26 November 2023. A request after that date is a request for an (ex officio) reduction (verzoek om ambtshalve vermindering).

### Example 8: Request for an (ex officio) reduction of the 2022 assessment (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: the online route needs fewer than 5 years after the tax year, and the material gives assessment 2020 until 31 December 2025 and assessment 2023 until 31 December 2028.

Step 1: the fifth year after 2022 is 2027.

Step 2: following the pattern of the material, the online request for a reduction for a 2022 assessment runs to 31 December 2027.

Step 3: after that date the request goes by letter.

### Example 9: Record-retention obligation (bewaarplicht) for a 4-year lease (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: the AWR term is 7 years and the term starts when the item (gegeven) is no longer current, and the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration example is a 4-year lease that is kept for those 4 years and then 7 more.

Step 1: the lease itself runs 4 years.

Step 2: the 7-year retention period (bewaartermijn) starts when the lease data are no longer actual.

Step 3: 4 + 7 = 11 years in total.

### Example 10: Chamber of Commerce (KVK) registration window (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: the statement (opgave) period is two weeks, starting one week before and ending one week after the start of the business.

Step 1: the business starts on 1 March 2022.

Step 2: one week before 1 March 2022 is 22 February 2022 (February 2022 has 28 days).

Step 3: one week after 1 March 2022 is 8 March 2022.

So the first registration (inschrijving) is made between 22 February 2022 and 8 March 2022, and the Chamber of Commerce issues the Chamber of Commerce number (KVK-nummer) immediately.

### Example 11: Assessment threshold (aanslaggrens) and refund threshold (teruggaafgrens) (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: the assessment threshold is € 48 and the refund threshold is € 15, and an amount is only paid if it is higher than the threshold.

Step 1: a computed final refund of € 14. € 14 is not higher than € 15, so no refund is paid.

Step 2: a computed final refund of € 20. € 20 is higher than € 15, so the refund is paid.

Step 3: if part of the refund was already received monthly through a provisional assessment before year end, those received amounts count toward the threshold. A paid provisional assessment does not count.

### Example 12: VAT deadline and the leniency (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: the monthly period November 2022 has deadline 31 December 2022, and there are 7 calendar days leniency (kalenderdagen coulance) before the € 68 penalty (boete) applies.

Step 1: the return and the payment must both be received by 31 December 2022.

Step 2: the leniency runs from 31 December 2022 for 7 days, to 7 January 2023.

Step 3: a return filed on 5 January 2023 is inside the leniency, so no penalty applies. A return filed on 10 January 2023 is outside the leniency, so the penalty of € 68 applies.

### Example 13: Filing without an invitation for 2022 (Calculated example)

Inputs from section 2: without a filing invitation letter (aangiftebrief) a return can be filed up to 5 years back, by 31 December of the fifth year after the tax year.

Step 1: the fifth year after 2022 is 2027.

Step 2: the last day is 31 December 2027.

A return for 2022 filed on 1 December 2027 is in time. A return filed on 1 January 2028 is outside the period.
