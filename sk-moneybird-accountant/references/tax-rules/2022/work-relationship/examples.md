## 2. WORKED EXAMPLES

<a id="4-worked-examples"></a>

### Example 1, Calculated example: the persons-treated-as-employees test

Inputs, invented: a self-employed person without employees (ZZP, zelfstandige zonder personeel) works for one client 2 days a week and is paid a rate well above 2/5 of the minimum wage.

Step 1, day test. The condition is 2 or more days a week for the same client (opdrachtgever). The self-employed person works 2 days a week, so the day test is met. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst.

Step 2, pay test. The condition is at least 2/5 of the minimum wage. 2/5 = 2 ÷ 5 = 0,4 = 40%. So the pay must be at least 40% of the minimum wage. The checked sources give no 2022 minimum wage amount, so no euro amount is computed here. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/wanneer-is-sprake-van-loondienst.

Result: the test for persons treated as employees (gelijkgestelden) can be met. The relationship is then a deemed employment relationship (fictieve dienstbetrekking) unless the parties exclude it in writing before the first payment, see 3.4.

### Example 2, Calculated example: the instruction (aanwijzing) timeline in 2022

Inputs, invented: a client and a contractor work in a way that is in fact an employment relationship (dienstbetrekking). The case is not in bad faith (kwaadwillend). The Dutch Tax and Customs Administration gives an instruction on 1 March 2022.

Step 1. The follow-up term is usually 3 months. 1 March 2022 plus 3 months is 1 June 2022. By that date the client can reshape the relationship so the contractor really works as a self-employed person (zelfstandige), or process it as an employment relationship in the payroll return (loonaangifte). Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.

Step 2. If the instruction is not followed, a correction obligation (correctieverplichting) applies from the date of the instruction. That date is 1 March 2022, not 1 June 2022. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.

### Example 3, Calculated example: model agreement validity

Inputs, invented: the parties work under an approved model agreement (modelovereenkomst) that was approved before 6 September 2024 and that states a validity date of 1 January 2027. They work exactly as described.

Step 1. No employment (loondienst), because an approved model agreement applies and the parties work as described. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/werken-met-modelovereenkomsten.

Step 2. The stop on 6 September 2024 does not end the approval. Approved agreements valid on 6 September 2024 can be used up to and including 31 December 2029, even if the agreement states another validity date. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/geen-nieuwe-modelovereenkomsten-meer.

Step 3. The stated date of 1 January 2027 therefore does not cut the use short. The end date is 31 December 2029.

Step 4. If the parties work differently, the agreement does not hold, because the qualification depends on how the work is actually done. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/geen-nieuwe-modelovereenkomsten-meer.

### Example 4, Official example: the € 36 from the explanatory memorandum

This example is taken from the explanatory memorandum (memorie van toelichting) as recorded in the checked sources. Source: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html.

Step 1. The WML (minimum wage) at 1 January 2025 is € 14,06 per hour.

Step 2. 120% of € 14,06 = 1,2 × € 14,06 = € 16,872.

Step 3. The surcharges (opslagen) are 25% for disability insurance (AOV) and pension and 15% for general costs, so 25% + 15% = 40%. € 16,872 × 1,40 = € 23,6208.

Step 4. Times 1,5 for non-billable hours: € 23,6208 × 1,5 = € 35,4312, so € 35,43 unrounded excluding VAT (excl. btw).

Step 5. Rounded up to whole euros: € 36,00. That is the € 36 in art. 7:610aa lid 1 BW.

### Example 5, Calculated example: the indexation to the reference date 1 January 2026

Step 1. The WML moves from € 14,06 to € 14,71. The change is € 14,71 - € 14,06 = € 0,65. Sources: https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2025 and https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2026.

Step 2. As a percentage of € 14,06: € 0,65 ÷ € 14,06 = 0,0462, so about 4,62%.

Step 3. The unrounded amount follows the WML percentage from € 35,43: € 35,43 × 1,0462 = € 37,0669, so about € 37,07. Source for the method: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html.

Step 4. Cross-check with the full method: € 14,71 × 1,2 = € 17,652. Then € 17,652 × 1,40 = € 24,7128. Then € 24,7128 × 1,5 = € 37,0692, also about € 37,07.

Step 5. Rounded up to whole euros: € 38. Note: the intermediate arithmetic is derived from the explanatory memorandum method and the WML figures in section 2. The € 38 itself is stated by the Senate (Eerste Kamer), rijksoverheid.nl and Ondernemersplein. Sources: https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36783_wet_invoering, https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/03/06/kabinet-kiest-voor-meer-rust-en-duidelijkheid-voor-zzpers-en-opdrachtgevers and https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/wetswijzigingen/laag-uurtarief-van-zzper-kan-leiden-tot-arbeidsovereenkomst/.

### Example 6, Calculated example: the presumption applied to two hourly rates

Inputs, invented: two engagements, one at € 30 per hour and one at € 40 per hour, both with a client acting in a profession or business. Assume the law is in force, which it is from 31 December 2026. Sources: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html and https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-207.html.

Step 1. Art. 7:610aa lid 1: pay of at most € 36 per hour gives the presumption. € 30 is at most € 36, so the presumption applies to the first engagement. Source: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html.

Step 2. The client must then prove there is no employment contract (arbeidsovereenkomst). Source: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html.

Step 3. € 40 is above € 36 and also above the indexed € 38, so the presumption does not apply to the second engagement on either amount. Sources: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html and https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-36783-3.html.

Step 4, exception. Lid 3: the presumption does not apply when the other party is a natural person not acting in a profession or business. Source: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-158.html.

Step 5, open point. The amount for the first application is given by ministerial regulation (ministeriële regeling) and was not found on 11 October 2026, so the exact first amount is not settled, see section 7.

### Example 7, Calculated example: BAZ at about 70%

Step 1. The docket summary states about 70% of income up to the minimum wage. Source: https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/wetsvoorstellen/detail?cfg=wetsvoorsteldetails&qry=wetsvoorstel%3A36912.

Step 2. Using the WML figure from section 2, € 14,06 per hour at 1 January 2025: 70% = 0,70 × € 14,06 = € 9,842, so about € 9,84 per hour. Source: https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2025.

Step 3. Using € 14,71 per hour at 1 January 2026: 0,70 × € 14,71 = € 10,297, so about € 10,30 per hour. Source: https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon/bedragen-minimumloon-2026.

Step 4. The waiting period (wachttijd) is about one year and the opt-out needs a qualifying private policy. Source: https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/wetsvoorstellen/detail?cfg=wetsvoorsteldetails&qry=wetsvoorstel%3A36912. Note: the percentage is "about 70%", and the WML figures are the section 2 figures, not 2022 amounts.

### Example 8, Calculated example: corrections after the moratorium

Step 1. From 1 January 2025 corrections go no further back than 1 January 2025. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.

Step 2. Exception: bad faith (kwaadwillendheid) or an instruction from before that date that was not followed allows up to 5 years back. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.

Step 3. Only in 2030 can the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration again go back the full 5 years. Check: 2030 - 5 years = 2025, which lines up with the 1 January 2025 limit. Source: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/arbeidsrelaties/content/handhaving.
